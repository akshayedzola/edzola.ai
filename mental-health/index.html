One Client ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Screen with care

Who screened positive and hasn't heard from us?

People say tension, a heavy heart, thinking too much. Nobody says depression. A screen only helps if every positive score, and every positive item 9, reaches someone who can respond that week.

Your team does

  • CHWs read the PHQ-9 aloud from an offline phone form, in the local language.
  • Scores add up on the phone, so nobody tallies items in front of the person.
  • Contact details go on a separate screen and are stored apart from scores.

The system does on its own

  • Any non-zero item 9 alerts the on-call supervisor by app and SMS.
  • A moderate or higher score creates an intake booking for the coordinator.
  • No intake 7 days after a positive screen? A gentle follow-up call is flagged.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • The PHQ-9 played in pre-recorded local-language audio
  • A monthly look at which sources turn screens into intakes
  • Sensitisation scripts adapted into local words for distress

Stage 02 · Intake & triage

What level of care does each person need?

The first one-on-one decides whether someone comes back. It's also where risk gets assessed properly and a safety plan is made together with the person.

Your team does

  • Counsellors do the intake on a PIN-locked tablet, in a private space.
  • The risk section can't be skipped. High risk holds the form until the supervisor knows.
  • Supervisors review triage daily, seeing only their own counsellors' clients.

The system does on its own

  • A high risk rating alerts the supervisor and clinician with a call-back task.
  • Specialist referrals create a task for the psychiatrist or district programme.
  • Anyone still unassigned after 5 days goes to the coordinator.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Group places suggested by language, location, gender and open slots
  • The approved triage protocol turned into a tablet checklist
  • Monthly waiting time from screen to first session, by district

Stage 03 · Sessions that work

Is the therapy helping, and is it delivered as designed?

Brief structured therapies work when they're delivered as designed. In the field, sessions get shorter, content drifts and groups thin out by week four.

Your team does

  • Counsellors log attendance and a quick check straight after the session, offline, in under three minutes.
  • Notes are short and structured, so nobody writes essays after a heavy group.
  • Supervisors rate fidelity from observed sessions or audio recorded with consent.

The system does on its own

  • Two missed sessions create a check-in call task.
  • No PHQ-9 improvement by session 6 prompts a supervisor case review.
  • Any rise in risk re-alerts the supervisor and reopens the safety plan.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Draft fidelity feedback from consented session audio
  • Early flags for people not improving or likely to drop out
  • Session handouts in local languages, from the approved manual

Stage 04 · Stay well

Who's still well at six months, and who's slipping?

End-of-therapy scores look good. What matters is whether people are still well three to six months later, back at work and managing their household.

Your team does

  • Follow-up calls and visits land in the counsellor's app at discharge.
  • Phone follow-ups open by checking it's a safe time to talk.
  • Peer facilitators log alumni group attendance only, never what was said.

The system does on its own

  • Three- and six-month follow-ups schedule themselves at discharge.
  • A score above threshold or any risk alerts the supervisor to offer a booster.
  • Anyone not reached after three tries goes to the supervisor, never closed silently.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • What's linked to lasting recovery: dose, fidelity, alumni groups
  • A draft funder report with totals and anonymised themes
  • Consented IVRS re-screens, PIN first, positives to a person
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Seven connected records: client, screening, risk assessment, care plan, session, counsellor, supervision
  • PHQ-9, GAD-7 and ENACT built in, with referral routes to psychiatrists, the district programme and Tele-MANAS
  • Names and phone numbers in a locked contact table, apart from scores and notes
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms read aloud in Marathi, Hindi or your language
  • A different screen for the screener, counsellor, supervisor and lead
  • Risk alerts and follow-ups that fire on their own
  • Screeners see that the person they screened started care
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Mental health data is some of the most private there is. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Chatbots for someone in distressA person with suicidal thoughts needs a trained human.
  • AI suicide risk scoresRisk is a clinical conversation. A false low could cost a life.
  • Triage without a supervisorA person decides the care tier, with context a form can't hold.
  • AI in place of a counsellorThe relationship is the therapy. Lay counsellors are the evidence.
  • Recording sessions without consentPeople stop speaking freely, and that trust doesn't come back.
  • Session content outside the care teamManagers and funders see totals, never what was said.
