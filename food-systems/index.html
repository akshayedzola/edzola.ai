One Enrolment ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Enrol and plan

Who's in, and how much do we need to order?

Enrolment runs on a hard calendar. Get a farm size or a school roll wrong here and every order, route and target downstream inherits it.

Your team does

  • Field officers enrol farmers at village meetings on an offline app.
  • Group leaders collect input orders. Coordinators confirm school rolls and agency demand after a site visit.
  • The programme manager approves lists and consolidated orders before procurement.

The system does on its own

  • Orders unconfirmed a week before cutoff alert the field officer and group leader.
  • A loan that looks too big for the farm gets flagged.
  • Partner agencies missing a hygiene or storage check can't be sent deliveries.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Voice enrolment in the local language for farmers who don't read
  • Likely duplicate farmers or households flagged across groups
  • Input demand forecast from past seasons and current enrolment

Stage 02 · Inputs and support

Did every farmer get the right inputs before sowing?

Seed on credit, a subsidy file at the horticulture office, a drip kit waiting on a sanction. If any of it lands two weeks late, the season's gone. The record shows which farmer is waiting on what, and at which desk.

Your team does

  • Field officers complete subsidy and loan checklists with the farmer, with a photo of each document.
  • Warehouse staff log every input lot at the dock, with a photo of the batch label.
  • Installers mark each site surveyed, installed and handed over, with photos.

The system does on its own

  • Files pending over 30 days at any office alert the field manager, with the desk and the date.
  • Lots near expiry, or failing a quality check, are locked from dispatch.
  • Farmers still without inputs 10 days before planting go on the field officer's list.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Subsidy papers and batch labels read from photos
  • Missing documents spotted before a file leaves for the office
  • Agronomy guides turned into short voice messages in local languages

Stage 03 · Through the season

Which farms are slipping this season, and why?

A missed training, late planting, a pest nobody reported. Advice lands best when it's timed to the crop stage, and field visits should go where the season is going wrong.

Your team does

  • Field officers log visits, crop stage and photos of problems on an offline app.
  • Farmers send voice notes or answer IVRS questions on crop stage and pests.
  • Agronomists review flagged farms and send advice by crop and stage.

The system does on its own

  • Advice goes out by crop stage, in the farmer's language, as voice or WhatsApp.
  • Planting more than 10 days late, or a pest photo, puts the farm on the next visit list.
  • Farms with no update in 21 days alert the field officer.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Pest and disease photos sorted for the agronomist to check first
  • Farmers' voice notes turned into crop-stage updates
  • A weekly look at late planting and pest reports by village

Stage 04 · Measure what changed

Did yields, repayments and attendance actually move?

For farmers it's yield, repayment and income across the season. For feeding it's attendance and food security. Both need measurement planned from the day of enrolment.

Your team does

  • Field officers record crop cuts and cash repayments on the offline app.
  • UPI repayments update the loan ledger on their own.
  • Schools share meal-day attendance by SMS or UDISE+ export. IVRS surveys collect feedback.

The system does on its own

  • Farmers more than 20% behind get an SMS and go on the field officer's visit list.
  • Follow-up FIES surveys link to each household's baseline.
  • Negative meal feedback creates a task for the kitchen manager.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Season-end yield and repayment by package, crop and district
  • Early flags for farmers who may struggle, for support visits
  • Season reports drafted from the outcome dashboards

What food systems teams ask us for first

Farm technology and enterprise

From the first call to the third harvest, one farmer record.

A lead comes in from a farmer champion or the call centre. The site survey, the subsidy file, the loan, the installation date and every crop cycle after that attach to the same farmer. Managers see where farmers drop out: after the survey, while the subsidy's pending, or after a bad first crop.

  • Lead source and conversion by village, champion and partner
  • Installation scheduled only once the subsidy's sanctioned and her share is paid
  • Each crop cycle with its own inputs, advice and harvest
  • Service requests after installation, with days to resolve
Subsidies and loans

Know which subsidy files are stuck, and at which desk.

Each farmer's file carries its checklist: land record, quotation, bank sanction, photos. The system shows when it went to the horticulture office, how many days it's been there and what came back. Files pending over 45 days go on the coordinator's list for an in-person visit, and the farmer gets an update in her language.

  • Document checklist that blocks submission until complete
  • Days pending by district office and scheme
  • Farmer's share, loan disbursement and subsidy release matched on one ledger
Working through partners

Your partners sign up farmers. You still see every farm.

Partner NGOs and franchisees log leads, installs and crop cycles in their own login. Your agronomists see every farm's advice history and your managers see results by partner. Partners see their own farmers, and nobody else's.

  • Partner-level funnel from lead to installed to harvesting
  • Shared definitions so every partner counts the same way
  • Role-based access for partners, agronomists and funders
School meals and food banks

Running a kitchen? The same record works per plate.

One Enrolment ID per school or partner agency. Every lot, batch cooked and drop is logged with time and GPS, and a cold-dal complaint opens a same-day kitchen check. Meal counts line up with your PM POSHAN returns.

  • Expiry and quality holds before dispatch
  • Drops confirmed by the school on SMS, IVRS or WhatsApp
  • Late and short deliveries by route, every week
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Six connected records: farmer, group or partner, input lot, crop cycle, loan or subsidy, outcome
  • Subsidy and loan files tracked to release, and for feeding programmes, meal counts that line up with PM POSHAN returns
  • FIES, dietary diversity, yield per acre and repayment set up from enrolment
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Kannada, Hindi, Swahili or your language
  • Drop times, GPS and signed-note photos on every delivery
  • A different screen for the driver, the kitchen, the field officer and the director
  • Schools and farmers get their own record back, by SMS
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Food, credit and children's health are on the line. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Farm size from satellite images aloneSmallholder plots are fragmented. A wrong estimate sets the wrong loan.
  • AI deciding who gets food aidWrongful exclusion means a family goes hungry.
  • AI agronomy advice sent straight to farmersGeneric advice ignores local soil and rain. It can cost a season.
  • Automated food safety sign-offA trained person checks smell, texture and temperature. Children eat this.
  • Collections messages that escalate on their ownA drought isn't a default. Pressure forces distress sales.
  • Children's height and weight in public AI toolsHealth measurements stay in your private system.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. Land records and bank details are visible only to the staff who process subsidy and loan files.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
