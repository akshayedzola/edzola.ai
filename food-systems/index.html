One Enrolment ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Enrol and plan

Who's in, and how much do we need to order?

Enrolment runs on a hard calendar. Get a farm size or a school roll wrong here and every order, route and target downstream inherits it.

Your team does

  • Field officers enrol farmers at village meetings on an offline app.
  • Group leaders collect input orders. Coordinators confirm school rolls and agency demand after a site visit.
  • The programme manager approves lists and consolidated orders before procurement.

The system does on its own

  • Orders unconfirmed a week before cutoff alert the field officer and group leader.
  • A loan that looks too big for the farm gets flagged.
  • Partner agencies missing a hygiene or storage check can't be sent deliveries.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Voice enrolment in the local language for farmers who don't read
  • Likely duplicate farmers or households flagged across groups
  • Input demand forecast from past seasons and current enrolment

Stage 02 · Source and cook

Is the right stock, safe to use, ready in time?

Inputs on credit timed to the season, or a kitchen cooking at scale to FSSAI norms. Both need the right quantity, of acceptable quality, with a record of where it came from.

Your team does

  • Warehouse staff log every lot at the dock, with a photo of the batch label.
  • Quality officers check each lot before it's released.
  • Kitchen managers enter production and waste per batch at the end of each shift.

The system does on its own

  • Lots within 7 days of expiry jump to the top of the dispatch list.
  • A failed quality check locks the lot.
  • Groups still without inputs 10 days before planting alert the field manager.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Batch labels and expiry dates read from photos
  • Tomorrow's meal quantities forecast from attendance and holidays
  • Agronomy guides turned into short voice messages in local languages

Stage 03 · Deliver on time

Did it arrive, on time and in good condition?

A hot meal has a window of a couple of hours. Seed has a couple of weeks. Records here prove what reached whom, and whether it made the window.

Your team does

  • Drivers mark each drop with time, GPS and a photo of the signed note.
  • Teachers and partner staff confirm receipt and condition by SMS, IVRS or WhatsApp.
  • The logistics lead watches routes live and reroutes a delayed van.

The system does on its own

  • A drop unconfirmed 30 minutes after schedule alerts the logistics lead.
  • A quality complaint from a school opens a same-day kitchen investigation.
  • Quantity gaps over 5% get reconciled before the next dispatch.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Routes planned around drop points, traffic and time windows
  • Signed delivery notes read from photos
  • A weekly look at late and short deliveries by route

Stage 04 · Measure what changed

Did yields, repayments and attendance actually move?

For farmers it's yield, repayment and income across the season. For feeding it's attendance and food security. Both need measurement planned from the day of enrolment.

Your team does

  • Field officers record crop cuts and cash repayments on the offline app.
  • UPI repayments update the loan ledger on their own.
  • Schools share meal-day attendance by SMS or UDISE+ export. IVRS surveys collect feedback.

The system does on its own

  • Farmers more than 20% behind get an SMS and go on the field officer's visit list.
  • Follow-up FIES surveys link to each household's baseline.
  • Negative meal feedback creates a task for the kitchen manager.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Season-end yield and repayment by package, crop and district
  • Early flags for farmers who may struggle, for support visits
  • Season reports drafted from the outcome dashboards
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Six connected records: enrollee, farmer group or partner agency, lot, distribution, loan, outcome
  • School rolls matched to UDISE+ codes, with meal counts that line up with PM POSHAN returns
  • FIES, dietary diversity, yield per acre and repayment set up from enrolment
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Kannada, Hindi, Swahili or your language
  • Drop times, GPS and signed-note photos on every delivery
  • A different screen for the driver, the kitchen, the field officer and the director
  • Schools and farmers get their own record back, by SMS
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Food, credit and children's health are on the line. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Farm size from satellite images aloneSmallholder plots are fragmented. A wrong estimate sets the wrong loan.
  • AI deciding who gets food aidWrongful exclusion means a family goes hungry.
  • AI agronomy advice sent straight to farmersGeneric advice ignores local soil and rain. It can cost a season.
  • Automated food safety sign-offA trained person checks smell, texture and temperature. Children eat this.
  • Collections messages that escalate on their ownA drought isn't a default. Pressure forces distress sales.
  • Children's height and weight in public AI toolsHealth measurements stay in your private system.
