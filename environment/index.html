At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Map with the village

Which parcels do we take up first?

If an outside surveyor draws the baseline, the village won't own the plan built on it. The community's map and the GIS layer have to describe the same land.

Your team does

  • Facilitators log consent meetings offline the same day, with attendance by hamlet and gender.
  • A GIS associate walks boundaries with local youth and gives each parcel its ID.
  • Trained villagers record quadrat and well readings, one form per parcel visit.

The system does on its own

  • No consent resolution attached? The parcel can't move to planning.
  • A boundary overlapping another parcel or a reserve forest alerts the coordinator.
  • Institutions with too few women office bearers get flagged.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Gram sabha recordings turned into draft minutes in the local language
  • Satellite imagery pre-sorts parcels before the field walk
  • The baseline form and GIS attribute table built from one field list

Stage 02 · Plan with the gram sabha

What goes on which parcel, this season, paid for by whom?

A plan that lives in a PDF at the district office can't be enforced. Convergence with MGNREGA and state schemes is where most of the money is, and where the paperwork breaks.

Your team does

  • The facilitator enters the micro-plan parcel by parcel during the planning meeting.
  • The institution secretary photographs signed bylaws and sends them on WhatsApp.
  • The coordinator checks MGNREGA sanction status every week. Passed plans are locked.

The system does on its own

  • A sanction still pending 45 days before planting reminds the coordinator.
  • Each village gets a printable plan sheet for the panchayat wall.
  • The institution hears 30 days before a conservation agreement is due for review.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • The MGNREGA work estimate drafted from the approved plan
  • Plan and bylaws translated into a plain-read wall version
  • Native species mixes suggested per parcel from soil and rainfall

Stage 03 · Plant and pay

What was planted where, and has everyone been paid?

Implementation is where numbers inflate. A log of 10,000 saplings with no GPS or species split won't survive an audit, and protection stops the week wages slip.

Your team does

  • Nursery stewards log batches weekly on a shared phone, with photos.
  • Site supervisors record planting and daily muster per parcel, with GPS captured automatically.
  • A programme officer checks one in ten plots on the ground before finance pays.

The system does on its own

  • A planting entry with GPS outside its parcel gets rejected.
  • More saplings planted than the nursery sent? The parcel gets flagged.
  • Any muster unpaid for 10 days alerts finance.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Saplings counted from drone images of a planted plot
  • Duplicate or improbable muster entries flagged for review
  • A weekly plain-language progress note for the district team

Stage 04 · Count what survives

What's still alive in year three, and who's looking after it?

A restoration programme is judged in year three. Carbon and biodiversity buyers will ask for survival plot by plot, and they'll check.

Your team does

  • Community monitors count live saplings in fixed plots at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months.
  • An ecologist repeats baseline transects twice a year. Seasonal NDVI lands in the same database.
  • The institution scores its own year with the facilitator, together.

The system does on its own

  • Parcels under 60% survival at 12 months go into next year's replanting plan.
  • A sharp NDVI drop between seasons alerts the institution and supervisor.
  • Survival counts schedule themselves from each planting date.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Canopy and land cover change per parcel from seasonal imagery
  • Birds and plants named from monitors' photos and audio
  • The carbon or funder report assembled from parcel data
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Six connected records: institution, parcel, micro-plan, works entry, steward, monitoring visit
  • Works tagged to MGNREGA, CAMPA or state watershed budgets, with sanction status
  • Survival, canopy and groundwater indicators set at baseline, parcel by parcel
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Telugu, Kannada, Hindi or your language
  • GPS and photos on every plot, checked against the parcel boundary
  • A different screen for the monitor, the supervisor and the programme head
  • The village gets its own map and survival chart back, on paper
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Land, rights and wages are on the line. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • AI-drawn boundaries as the parcel of recordBoundaries are a social agreement. A clean polygon ignores customary use.
  • Reading consent from attendance numbersConsent is about who spoke and who objected. A model can't judge that.
  • AI writing the grazing rulesRules the gram sabha didn't argue over won't be enforced.
  • Letting AI decide who gets paidA wrong hold means a family misses wages. A person decides.
  • Survival from satellite images aloneYoung saplings don't show at Sentinel resolution. Only plot counts tell you.
  • Stewards' bank details in public AI toolsWage and identity data stays in your private system.
