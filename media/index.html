One Story ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Tips and pitches

Which pitches and claims do we take on today?

Stringers in districts others ignore are the whole point. If you don't know who covers which block, in what language, the story budget is a wish list.

Your team does

  • Reporters pitch through a phone form or WhatsApp bot, and each pitch gets a Story ID.
  • The monitoring desk logs claims from the tipline and social feeds every shift.
  • The news editor assigns stories in the daily meeting. Source contacts stay in a reporter-only field.

The system does on its own

  • Every reader tip gets a WhatsApp reply with a reference number.
  • A pitch unassigned for 48 hours alerts the editor.
  • Districts with no pitches in 30 days are flagged to the bureau chief.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Duplicate WhatsApp tips clustered into one claim
  • Checkable statements flagged in speech transcripts
  • The best-placed reporter suggested by district, language and load

Stage 02 · Check and clear

Is this ready to publish, and in which languages?

Verification is the product. A wrong verdict or a missed legal flag costs more credibility than a year of good work earns.

Your team does

  • Reporters file copy, video or audio against the Story ID from a phone, and uploads resume when the signal drops.
  • Fact-checkers log each claim and its evidence before editing starts, with a rating if you publish verdicts.
  • Editor and legal reviewer sign off on the board. Translators work from the locked master.

The system does on its own

  • Publish stays blocked until right of reply and legal clearance are logged.
  • A False or Misleading verdict about an official needs a second checker.
  • A claim still trending after 24 hours in checking alerts the editor.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Past fact-checks searched for the same claim
  • First-pass translations of cleared copy
  • Field interviews transcribed in local languages

Stage 03 · Reach every channel

Who saw it, online and off?

Much of your audience meets the story on a WhatsApp forward, on community radio or on a projector in the panchayat hall. Web analytics see a fraction of that.

Your team does

  • YouTube and Facebook views sync to the Story ID after publish. The audience team logs radio and screenings by hand.
  • Local reporters log screening attendance and discussion on a phone form that evening.
  • The engagement desk logs tips against the story that triggered them.

The system does on its own

  • Partner outlets get a WhatsApp alert with cleared assets on publish.
  • Tips tagged as claims go straight to the monitoring desk.
  • A weekly reach summary per reporter goes to the team channel.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A cleared story cut into WhatsApp card, caption and audio script drafts
  • A weekly read on which formats carry which beats
  • Draft replies to common tipline questions

Stage 04 · Prove what changed

Which stories changed something, and can we show it?

Impact is a collector's order, a corrected figure, a claim retracted. It's rare and slow, and it has to be logged the week it happens or it's gone.

Your team does

  • Reporters log impact on a quick phone form, with a photo of the order or a screenshot.
  • The standards editor reviews impact monthly and rates how strong the link is.
  • The revenue team manages members, subscriptions, commissioned work and grants against beats and stories.

The system does on its own

  • Reporters get a prompt at 30 and 90 days to log any follow-up.
  • The revenue team gets the beat's impact log 45 days before a grant report is due.
  • Lapsed members are flagged for renewal after 30 days.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Government orders and assembly questions scanned for your stories
  • Funder reports drafted from the verified impact log
  • Retention cohorts showing which beats bring readers who stay

What media & journalism teams ask us for first

Reporter safety

Your reporters' safety, logged like it matters

Reporters covering caste, violence and local power take risks the story sheet never shows. Sensitive assignments get a check-in schedule, and a missed check-in alerts the bureau chief. Threats or harassment are logged privately, with what the newsroom did about it and when.

  • Sensitive-story flag set at assignment
  • Check-ins by WhatsApp or a missed call
  • Incident log visible only to the editor and safety lead
  • Legal and support follow-ups with due dates
Video newsrooms

From a phone in the village to YouTube, one record

Reporters upload rushes from the field, and the upload picks up where it left off when the signal drops. The Story ID follows the file into the edit queue and onto every platform. Once it's live, YouTube and Facebook views pull in against the story, so you can see which districts' reporting your audience actually watches.

  • Upload status and edit queue by story
  • Titles, thumbnails and captions tracked per platform
  • Views and watch time by district, beat and reporter
  • Follow-up prompts at 30 and 90 days
Building your network

From trainee to trusted stringer, without losing track

Each contributor keeps one ID from the first training module to their hundredth story. Course progress, mentor feedback, pitches taken up, payments and safety check-ins sit together, so editors know who's ready for a harder assignment and who needs support.

  • Course modules and assignments completed
  • First pitches, edits and published stories
  • Stipends and story payments, with dates
  • Districts where new reporters are needed
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Connected records for contributor, story, claim, source, video and channel metric, and impact event
  • Source identities in a restricted field, visible only to the assigned reporter and editor
  • Indicators funders ask for, from hours to verdict to impact events by beat
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Pitches, tips and screening logs from a phone or WhatsApp, in your languages
  • A different screen for the stringer, the checker, the editor and the revenue team
  • Publish blocks, reply prompts and 90-day impact reminders that fire on their own
  • Stringers see which pitches were taken and when they'll be paid
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Your sources trust you with their safety. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Source identities in any AI toolOne leak can get a source hurt. Source data stays encrypted and off every model.
  • AI-assigned verdictsA verdict carries your newsroom's name. A checker reasons it from evidence.
  • Publishing one deepfake detector's resultDetectors get it wrong both ways. Treat them as a lead, then verify provenance and sources.
  • AI copy under a reporter's bylineReaders trust bylines. It breaks that trust and your code of ethics.
  • Fully automated tipline repliesTipsters sometimes share danger or abuse. A person reads what comes in.
  • Member lists in public AI toolsDonor identity can expose supporters of independent media to pressure.
What changes, and for whom

Everyone enters less and gets something back.

Stringer

Knows when they'll be paid.

Pitches taken up, deadlines, payment status and how their story did in their own district.

Fact-checker

Starts from what's already checked.

Past verdicts on similar claims, the evidence trail, and which of their own verdicts got challenged.

News editor

Runs the meeting from one board.

Pitches waiting, claims trending, reporter workload and districts that have gone quiet.

Audience editor

Counts the audience analytics miss.

Web, WhatsApp, radio and screenings against one Story ID, with the tips each story brought in.

Editor-in-chief & funders

Get impact they can stand behind.

Impact events with evidence attached, and an honest rating of how much the story caused.

A story that ran before a policy changed didn't necessarily cause it. Every impact event carries a contribution rating from your standards editor, and weak links get reported as weak.

Funders notice overclaiming. They notice honesty too.
