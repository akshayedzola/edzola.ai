06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit use the skilling placement and retention tracker?

A skilling placement and retention tracker should separate placement claim, verification evidence, current employment state, follow-up attempts and reviewer decisions. It is a practical starting point for smaller cohorts and a clear migration source when a full MIS becomes necessary.

What fields or sections should the skilling placement and retention tracker contain?

Start with candidate placement and follow-up row. The minimum useful record set covers candidate and course identifiers, placement claim and start date, evidence and verification state, 30, 90 and 180-day follow-up, job change, exit and reason and next action, owner and review note. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Who should complete and maintain the skilling placement and retention tracker?

The team that owns candidate placement and follow-up row should maintain the resource, with input from frontline, programme, MEL or data, technology and leadership roles as relevant. Keep one named owner responsible for version control, review decisions and updates.

How can the skilling placement and retention tracker be adapted for different programme sizes?

Keep the definition of candidate placement and follow-up row stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.

How can a team check that the skilling placement and retention tracker is complete and useful?

Keep the source, definition, time, owner, status and review decision together. Monitor rows with complete identifiers, verified placement coverage, follow-ups due and completed and nonresponse retained in reporting, and preserve limitations instead of cleaning them out of the report.
