06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit use the nonprofit MIS cost estimator?

A nonprofit MIS cost estimator should account for workflow complexity, user roles, data migration, integrations, offline use, security, training and ongoing ownership. It should return a range with assumptions and risk drivers rather than a false fixed quote.

What fields or sections should the nonprofit MIS cost estimator contain?

Start with scoped estimate with assumptions. The minimum useful record set covers programme and workflow count, roles, users and permission complexity, forms, reports and languages, migration volume and quality, integrations, offline and attachments and training, support and ownership period. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Who should complete and maintain the nonprofit MIS cost estimator?

The team that owns scoped estimate with assumptions should maintain the resource, with input from frontline, programme, MEL or data, technology and leadership roles as relevant. Keep one named owner responsible for version control, review decisions and updates.

How can the nonprofit MIS cost estimator be adapted for different programme sizes?

Keep the definition of scoped estimate with assumptions stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.

How can a team check that the nonprofit MIS cost estimator is complete and useful?

Keep the source, definition, time, owner, status and review decision together. Monitor assumptions explicitly answered, high-risk drivers identified, range sensitivity visible and actual cost compared after implementation, and preserve limitations instead of cleaning them out of the report.
