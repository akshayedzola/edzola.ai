06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit use the Theory of Change to data model guide?

Moving from a Theory of Change to a data model means identifying the actors, activities, outputs, outcomes, assumptions and decisions that need records—then removing everything the programme cannot collect or use responsibly. The result is not a database version of every box in the diagram.

What fields or sections should the Theory of Change to data model guide contain?

Start with outcome claim linked to programme evidence. The minimum useful record set covers actor and unit of analysis, activity and service delivery, output and immediate result, outcome observation and timing, assumption, risk and context and indicator, source and decision owner. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Who should complete and maintain the Theory of Change to data model guide?

The team that owns outcome claim linked to programme evidence should maintain the resource, with input from frontline, programme, MEL or data, technology and leadership roles as relevant. Keep one named owner responsible for version control, review decisions and updates.

How can the Theory of Change to data model guide be adapted for different programme sizes?

Keep the definition of outcome claim linked to programme evidence stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.

How can a team check that the Theory of Change to data model guide is complete and useful?

Keep the source, definition, time, owner, status and review decision together. Monitor outcomes linked to source records, indicators with owners and definitions, assumptions reviewed and unused collection removed, and preserve limitations instead of cleaning them out of the report.
