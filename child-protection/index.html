One Child Case ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Call to case

Which reports need someone there tonight?

The first record decides how fast someone gets there. Log too little and the field team goes in blind. Log too much and you've recorded a disclosure the child hasn't agreed to share.

Your team does

  • Helpline counsellors log calls live on a structured triage screen.
  • Outreach workers and CCPC volunteers report on an offline form or a monitored WhatsApp number.
  • The coordinator clears the open queue every shift and sets deadlines by danger rating.

The system does on its own

  • No field update 60 minutes after an emergency report? The coordinator's phone rings.
  • A sexual abuse report can't close until the POCSO reporting checklist is done.
  • Reports past their verification deadline show in red on the next shift's handover.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Regional-language calls turned into draft triage fields
  • Possible earlier cases suggested by place, age and description
  • A weekly hotspot summary by location and category

Stage 02 · Ready for the CWC

Does the CWC have what it needs to decide?

The JJ Act requires a rescued child to be produced before the CWC within 24 hours. A thin file means a placement made on guesswork, so every fact collected once should serve everyone who needs it.

Your team does

  • Field workers record rescue details offline at the site, with agencies present and GD or FIR numbers.
  • Caseworkers build the social investigation over several sessions, entering the child's account once.
  • CWC orders are photographed and attached the same day, with type and date keyed in.

The system does on its own

  • No CWC production logged by hour 18? An alert fires.
  • Inter-state cases create a family tracing task for the home district partner.
  • A high risk rating needs supervisor sign-off before the placement can be saved.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A draft social investigation report from the caseworker's fields
  • CWC order type, date and directions read from a photo
  • What's missing for each upcoming hearing, listed

Stage 03 · Care that moves

Which needs in the care plan are still unmet?

The Individual Care Plan is meant to drive every intervention. In practice it's written at admission and opened again at inspection, while progress sits in three staff diaries.

Your team does

  • Caseworkers update goals in review meetings, with the child present where appropriate.
  • Counsellors keep notes in a locked module. The wider team sees only a risk flag and a session count.
  • Home staff log school attendance and health events daily on a shared phone form.

The system does on its own

  • Reviews schedule themselves by risk level, with reminders to all three reviewers.
  • A counsellor's risk flag alerts the supervisor at once and opens a safety check.
  • 120 days in care with no restoration or alternative plan flags the CWC liaison.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A draft quarterly progress report to the CWC
  • A monthly look at length of stay and restoration readiness
  • Care plan summaries in the family's language

Stage 04 · Home, and safe

Is the child still safe a year after going home, or after turning 18?

Restoration and leaving care at 18 are where most files go quiet. Children found back at work or re-trafficked are usually the ones whose families never got the scheme support promised.

Your team does

  • Field workers or VCPC members log visits offline, with a required child-seen-alone check.
  • Phone follow-ups for distant restorations use the same visit form.
  • The supervisor reviews closure requests weekly against risk and visit history.

The system does on its own

  • A follow-up visit overdue by 14 days goes to the supervisor.
  • A new call or rescue matching a restored child alerts the original caseworker at once.
  • Near the end of the follow-up period, a closure review task opens. For children in long-term care, the aftercare plan is due at 17. For children in long-term care, the aftercare plan is due at 17. For children in long-term care, the aftercare plan is due at 17.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Consented IVRS check-ins between home visits
  • Re-entry patterns by district and scheme, for advocacy
  • Scheme forms pre-filled from the family record

What child protection teams ask us for first

Leaving care

Still with them at 18, 19 and 21.

Most homes lose track of young people the week they leave. The system opens an aftercare plan at 17, checks the documents they'll need, and follows education, work, housing and aftercare support with a named mentor until 21. Anyone out of contact for 60 days goes to the aftercare coordinator.

  • Pre-exit checklist: Aadhaar, bank account, certificates, school records
  • Mission Vatsalya aftercare support applications and payments tracked
  • Monthly mentor or peer check-in, logged in two minutes
  • Outcomes at 21: studying, working, housed, in contact
Long-term care

Every child's progress, in every home, on one screen.

Children stay for years, and their files grow across school, health, therapy and family contact. The Individual Care Plan sets goals by domain, reviews book themselves, and home staff log progress where they already log attendance. Head office sees every home by domain. Each home's team sees only its own children.

  • ICP goals for education, health, emotional wellbeing, life skills and family contact
  • School results, board exams and health checks on the same file
  • House parent and mentor visits and family contact logged
  • Restoration readiness reviewed at every ICP review
Registration and compliance

Inspection day is just another day.

The registers and reports a registered home must keep come from what staff enter every day. CWC progress reports, Management Committee and Children's Committee minutes, and Mission Vatsalya grant reports are drafted from the same records. Sponsors get progress updates that never reveal who the child is, as Section 74 of the JJ Act requires.

  • JJ Model Rules registers and ICP forms generated from daily entries
  • Quarterly CWC progress reports drafted for the caseworker to sign
  • Committee meetings, decisions and follow-ups on record
  • Sponsor updates with photos and names checked before anything leaves
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Eight connected records: child, case, incident or call, statutory order, care plan, family, follow-up visit, aftercare plan
  • Built around JJ Act timelines, JJ Model Rules forms, CWC and JJB orders, POCSO reporting, and Mission Vatsalya sponsorship and aftercare support
  • Identity details in a restricted block, visible only to the child's case team
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Bengali, Hindi or your language
  • A different screen for the counsellor, caseworker, house mother or mentor, supervisor and CWC liaison
  • Deadlines and escalations that fire on their own
  • Outreach workers hear when a child they reported is safe, with no case details
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

A child's file follows them for years. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Automated dispatch decisionsChildren understate danger. A missed emergency can't be undone.
  • Chatbots on the helplineA child in distress needs a trained human voice.
  • Risk scores for children or familiesThey repeat past bias and turn poverty into a label.
  • Age estimation from photosAge under the JJ Act follows the documentary or medical process.
  • AI reading counselling notesSession notes stay with the counsellor and their supervisor.
  • Auto-closing casesClosure is a judgement about safety, made on the whole file.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. Section 74 of the JJ Act applies: a child's identity is never shown to sponsors, donors or the public, and counselling notes are visible only to the counsellor and supervisor.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
