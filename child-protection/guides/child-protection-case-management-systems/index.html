06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit implement child protection case management systems?

Use a phased sequence: define access and escalation before configuration, model the case and review cycle, separate allegations from findings, test partner handoffs, monitor overdue safety actions and audit access and closure decisions. Each stage needs a named owner and a review gate before the next stage begins.

What records and data fields are needed for child protection case management systems?

Start with child protection case. The minimum useful record set covers child identity and safe relationships, concern, intake and source, assessment and safety plan, action, referral and responsible person, review, change and evidence and closure, transition and restricted archive. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Which staff roles and review steps should child protection case management systems include?

Give every stage a named operational owner and require a separate human review for best-interest determination, risk and safeguarding response, placement or removal decisions and case closure and disclosure. Use urgent actions acknowledged, reviews completed on time, referrals confirmed and access and export events reviewed to decide whether the workflow is ready to expand.

What are the most common mistakes when setting up child protection case management systems?

The main risks are broad access to narrative notes, automatic risk scoring used as judgement, referrals without confirmed ownership and records continuing beyond a justified retention period. Test these with representative cases and keep the consequential judgement with a named person.

How should child protection case management systems change for a small NGO versus a multi-state programme?

Keep the definition of child protection case stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.
