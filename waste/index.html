Six common operating models

Find the model closest to how your work actually runs.

Many organisations combine two or three. Start with the closest match, then borrow only the parts that solve a real programme problem.

Model 01

Waste-picker cooperative

Workers organise, receive services and earn through collection, sorting or sale.

Usually organised around
Workers, households, routes, attendance, materials, sales, payments and welfare.
Watch for
Worker identity, earnings and benefits are not the same as tonnage records.
Start with
Worker, shift or route, material, sale and payment.
Model 02

Municipal service or PPP

An organisation delivers collection or processing against ward or city service obligations.

Usually organised around
Zones, routes, vehicles, households, schedules, SLAs, complaints and invoices.
Watch for
Contract evidence and citizen service must connect to operational reality.
Start with
Service area, route, run, exception and SLA.
Model 03

Decentralised collection

Neighbourhood teams collect segregated waste from homes and institutions.

Usually organised around
Customers, properties, subscriptions, schedules, fees, pickups and exceptions.
Watch for
A route plan must adapt to missed pickups, access and real volumes.
Start with
Property, service plan, pickup, fee and complaint.
Model 04

Material recovery facility

A facility receives, sorts, stores and dispatches multiple material streams.

Usually organised around
Inbound loads, batches, weights, grades, inventory, buyers and dispatch.
Watch for
Mass balance, contamination and worker safety matter more than gross intake.
Start with
Inbound batch, sorting output, inventory and sale.
Model 05

Circular enterprise and EPR

Enterprises recover or transform material and report traceability to brands or authorities.

Usually organised around
Suppliers, products, batches, processing, certificates, buyers and obligations.
Watch for
Traceability claims require evidence across each custody handoff.
Start with
Material batch, custody event, output and EPR claim.
Model 06

Segregation and behaviour change

Teams help residents, schools or businesses adopt segregation and responsible disposal.

Usually organised around
Audiences, properties, sessions, nudges, audits, feedback and repeat behaviour.
Watch for
Awareness delivered is not behaviour sustained.
Start with
Property or group, intervention, waste audit and follow-up.

Not sure which one you are? Bring one tracker and describe a normal week. The model usually becomes clear quickly.

One worked example · Worker formalisation and material recovery
Follow one programme journey

For a collection and recovery programme, four questions connect service quality, worker wellbeing and material value.

This is one operating model, not a template for every waste & circularity organisation. Use it to see how everyday work, follow-up and management decisions can connect.

Stage 01 · Count every worker

Who's a waste picker, and who's still missing?

Waste pickers who aren't on a register lose access when a city contracts out collection. The ID card is what gets them through the DWCC gate and into the service agreement.

Your team does

  • Community surveyors enumerate in pairs at sites before picking starts, offline, with photos.
  • The cooperative committee verifies members at a weekly peer meeting.
  • A data associate removes duplicates before the list goes to the municipal health officer.

The system does on its own

  • Possible duplicates across wards get flagged by name, age and photo.
  • Workers get an SMS when their ID card is ready.
  • Cards due for renewal remind the organiser 60 days ahead.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Spoken answers in the local language fill the enumeration form
  • Duplicate matches suggested across enumeration rounds
  • The municipal MoU annexure drafted from the verified list

Stage 02 · Weigh by stream

How much did each ward recover, stream by stream?

The city pays on tonnage, or households and shops pay a monthly fee. Waste pickers earn on what's recoverable. Without weights by stream at the gate, you can't prove segregation is improving.

Your team does

  • Collection staff mark households covered, segregating and paid up per lane on an icon-based app or tally card.
  • The DWCC operator weighs every load at the gate as dry, wet, reject and sanitary.
  • Mobilisers log awareness visits against the lane they visited.

The system does on its own

  • A lane under 50% segregation for two weeks gets an awareness drive.
  • A route with no weighment by shift cut-off alerts the supervisor.
  • The ward officer or panchayat gets a weekly tonnage, coverage and fee summary.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Scale displays read from photos to fill weighments
  • Weekly routes suggested from coverage and tonnage
  • A ward summary of segregation trends for the corporation meeting

Stage 03 · Sort, sell, trace

Can every tonne sold be traced back to a ward?

Grade decides price, and brands buying EPR credits need a chain from DWCC to recycler. If your ledger can't show it, the EPR value goes to the aggregator.

Your team does

  • The shed supervisor logs each sorter's kg per grade at the close of shift.
  • The operator tags bales with QR labels and scans them at baling and dispatch.
  • The treasurer logs each sale with a photo of the weighbridge slip. The EPR coordinator reconciles monthly.

The system does on its own

  • Stock over shed capacity or held 30 days alerts the operator.
  • A sale to a recycler whose CPCB registration has lapsed gets flagged.
  • Every member's monthly earnings slip builds itself from sales and shifts.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Weighbridge slips and invoices read into sale records
  • Recycler rate trends by grade, with a hold or sell suggestion
  • Monthly EPR traceability packs compiled from bales and sales

Stage 04 · Income and cover

Are workers earning more, and is their cover still active?

Integration means steady income, insurance that pays out and children in school. The city needs diversion numbers too, and waste pickers should get the credit for them.

Your team does

  • Facilitators log scheme applications at camps and update them as approvals come in.
  • The treasurer enters each member's monthly income from sale shares and city payments.
  • Peer surveyors run the annual wellbeing follow-up, linked to the Worker ID.

The system does on its own

  • The worker and facilitator hear 30 days before an insurance renewal.
  • Income down more than 30% month on month flags the worker.
  • The quarterly diversion report for the corporation compiles itself.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Scheme eligibility checked from each worker's profile
  • A quarterly impact note from income, cover and diversion data
  • Scheme application forms pre-filled for the facilitator
Build your sector playbook

Choose the parts your programme needs, not a giant system all at once.

Choose modules according to who you serve and where you hold responsibility. A behaviour-change team needs less operational detail than an MRF or municipal contractor.

Customer, route and service CRM

Plan properties, schedules, teams, vehicles, pickups, fees and service exceptions.

Service levels and complaints

Connect citizen issues, contract SLAs, action owners, closure and verification.

Material batches and traceability

Follow source, weight, grade, custody, processing, inventory and destination.

MRF and inventory operations

Manage receiving, sorting, storage, quality, dispatch, buyers and reconciliation.

Worker welfare and safety

Track membership, PPE, benefits, incidents, training, payments and support.

EPR, buyers and revenue

Link obligations, certificates, invoices, payments and audit-ready evidence.

Questions the system should answer

Useful on an ordinary workday

  • Which property, route, worker or material batch is involved?
  • Was the service completed safely and on schedule?
  • Where did the material go, and what value was realised?
  • Which complaint, welfare issue or contract obligation is still open?
Measures worth seeing together

Delivery beside outcomes

  • Collection reliability and segregation
  • Tonnes recovered by verified stream
  • Material revenue and payment time
  • PPE, benefits and safety incidents
  • Complaint and SLA closure
  • EPR traceability and mass balance
Keep people in charge

Boundaries to agree before build

  • Workers participate in service and safety decisions
  • Personal welfare records stay separate from buyer reports
  • Traceability claims require auditable evidence
  • Automation never hides unsafe workloads or unpaid work
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Connected records for worker, household or business, ward or panchayat, collection log, recovery centre, sale and benefit enrolment
  • Occupational IDs under the SWM Rules, user-fee accounts for households and businesses, and recyclers checked against CPCB registration
  • Links to e-Shram, PM-JAY, PMJJBY and PMSBY tracked with renewal dates
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Icon-based screens that work offline, in Hindi, Kannada, Tamil or your language
  • QR-tagged bales and photos of every weighbridge slip
  • A different screen for the waste picker, the operator, the treasurer and the city
  • Every member gets an earnings slip back, every month
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, links to government IDs and AI features are set up to suit each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

This is a vulnerable workforce's data. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Face recognition to verify workersIt turns a dignity document into surveillance, and it fails most on the people you're trying to include.
  • The worker registry in public AI toolsNames, photos and phone numbers stay in your own system.
  • Cameras on household binsWaste pickers are trusted service providers. They shouldn't become enforcers.
  • AI setting the worker share splitFairness between sorters and collectors is the cooperative's call.
  • EPR claims from estimatesOverclaimed tonnage breaks the rules and the brand's trust.
  • Credit scores from income dataIrregular income is the nature of the work. A score locks workers out of loans.
What changes, and for whom

Everyone enters less and gets something back.

Waste picker

Sees what their lanes earned.

Kg collected on their route this week, segregation on their lanes, and their share.

DWCC operator

Knows the shed before it overflows.

Today's inbound by stream, stock by grade, days held and the last three rates offered.

Cooperative committee

Walks into the general body meeting with numbers.

Member income, benefit coverage and sale proceeds, ready to share and to negotiate with.

Programme manager

Sees which wards and workers need follow-up.

Segregation by ward, pending ID cards and every policy expiring in the next 60 days.

Leadership, city & brands

Get tonnes they can trace.

Diversion by ward credited to waste pickers, and EPR tonnage with the bale-to-recycler chain.

If the workers who dropped out aren't counted, median income looks better than your programme is. We report income for everyone who was carded, including those who left.

Cities and brands notice. It's what makes your numbers worth paying for.
