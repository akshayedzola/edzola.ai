Notice disengagement early enough for a safe response.

The direct answer

An attendance early-warning system should combine recent absence, attendance trend, transition points and known context into a review queue. It should prompt a trained person to investigate; it should not label a child as a dropout or predict intent automatically.

06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit implement school attendance and dropout early-warning systems?

Use a phased sequence: define absence and dropout carefully, use the least burdensome attendance source, set review thresholds with field teams, test false positives by subgroup, assign safe follow-up and close with evidence and reason. Each stage needs a named owner and a review gate before the next stage begins.

What records and data fields are needed for school attendance and dropout early-warning systems?

Start with learner attendance and follow-up episode. The minimum useful record set covers learner, school and class, session or daily attendance, reason and source of absence, transition or risk context, follow-up attempt and outcome and return, transfer, exit or unresolved status. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Which staff roles and review steps should school attendance and dropout early-warning systems include?

Give every stage a named operational owner and require a separate human review for whether and how to contact a family, safeguarding response, confirming transfer or dropout and changing support for a child. Use time from threshold to review, successful safe contact, documented return, transfer or exit and false-positive and unresolved rates to decide whether the workflow is ready to expand.

What are the most common mistakes when setting up school attendance and dropout early-warning systems?

The main risks are paper registers entered twice, punitive use of risk scores, missing transfers counted as dropout and contact that exposes sensitive family information. Test these with representative cases and keep the consequential judgement with a named person.

How should school attendance and dropout early-warning systems change for a small NGO versus a multi-state programme?

Keep the definition of learner attendance and follow-up episode stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.
