At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Find every child

Who's missing from the school register?

Seasonal migrants, girls kept home after Class 5, children working in the fields. If the survey stops at the main road, so does your enrolment drive.

Your team does

  • Volunteers survey every household on a shared phone, offline, in the local language.
  • The coordinator reviews flagged children each evening and assigns visits.
  • Head teachers confirm admission. SMC members get their hamlet's list on WhatsApp.

The system does on its own

  • No visit in 14 days? The volunteer gets a reminder.
  • A duplicate check runs before any new Student ID is issued.
  • Once admission is confirmed, the SMC list updates itself.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A voice note in Hindi becomes a tagged 'reason not in school'
  • A plain-language hamlet summary for the block manager, every week
  • The survey form translated into Marathi or Kannada from the agreed list

Stage 02 · Group by level

Which group should each child sit in?

A grade label hides the learning level. A Class 5 child reading at letter level needs a different room from one reading stories.

Your team does

  • Facilitators assess each child one-on-one in the first two weeks.
  • Mentors re-check one in ten children with a different assessor.
  • The system suggests a group. The facilitator confirms it.

The system does on its own

  • Saving a baseline proposes a group by level and school.
  • A big back-check disagreement alerts the mentor.
  • Support requests waiting over 10 days escalate.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Reading fluency estimated while a child reads a passage aloud
  • A first draft of learning groups, balanced by level and size
  • A baseline summary for the district office, by cluster

Stage 03 · Catch who slips

Who's slipping this week?

A monthly attendance total arrives too late. By then, the family has already left for the kiln.

Your team does

  • Facilitators mark attendance at the end of each session.
  • Mentors use the same short observation rubric on every visit.
  • Volunteers log absence calls and home visits in the same app.

The system does on its own

  • Three absences in a row create a follow-up task.
  • Attendance under 60% for two weeks alerts the mentor.
  • No observation in 30 days puts a facilitator on the visit list.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A photo of a paper register turned into digital attendance
  • Two coaching points suggested from a facilitator's last three observations
  • A weekly digest of groups where attendance is falling, and why

Stage 04 · Prove it & follow through

Did they learn, and did they reach the next school?

Endline scores only count if they stay connected to the child. Keep the case open until she's on the next school's register.

Your team does

  • Facilitators repeat the same tool at midline and endline.
  • External assessors retest a random 10 to 15% sample.
  • Coordinators verify admissions at the new school in June and July.

The system does on its own

  • Children moving to Class 6 or 9 get a July tracking task.
  • No status by 31 July? The child goes on a volunteer visit list.
  • Large gaps between assessors flag a school for review.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • The year-end outcomes report drafted from matched baseline and endline
  • Children flagged for low attendance, no gain and a transition coming up
  • Consented WhatsApp or IVRS check-ins with alumni families
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Seven connected records: child, household, school, group, assessment, facilitator, session
  • Mapped to UDISE+ codes so you can match against government data
  • Indicators your funder asks for, defined before the first form goes out
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Hindi, Marathi, Kannada or your language
  • A different screen for the volunteer, the mentor and the manager
  • Reminders and follow-ups that fire on their own
  • Something useful flows back to the frontline, every week
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Children's data needs care. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Deciding which child to skipEnrolment is a right. Every child identified gets a visit.
  • Guessing a disability from scoresThat's a screening by a trained professional.
  • Facial recognition for attendanceNot on children. A tick on a phone works fine.
  • Rating facilitators for appraisalsObservations exist to help people teach better.
  • Filling in missing endline scoresA gap gets reported as a gap.
  • Children's details in public chatbotsNames, phones and locations stay in your private system.
