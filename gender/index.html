One Member ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Reach every woman

Who hasn't the programme reached yet?

The women who come first to a meeting are rarely the ones with least voice. Widows, Dalit and Adivasi women, women with disabilities and young brides are often kept at home.

Your team does

  • Mobilisers list every household on an offline phone form and sync at the block office.
  • Enumerators run the baseline privately, in her language, and note that nobody else was listening.
  • Coordinators share the priority list at a community meeting before groups form.

The system does on its own

  • A baseline marked 'not private' goes back for a re-interview.
  • Low priority-group enrolment in a village triggers a second listing round.
  • If she withdraws consent, her record locks and drops off every active list.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A voice baseline for women who can't read, with the enumerator beside her
  • Likely duplicate enrolments flagged across villages
  • Baseline results summarised by village and social group

Stage 02 · Keep women coming

Who's dropping off, and why?

Attendance falls in harvest season and after the first session on violence, when some husbands push back. The right response depends on whether it's the calendar, the content or the backlash.

Your team does

  • Facilitators mark attendance by Member ID offline, or photograph the paper register the same day.
  • A quick oral or picture check after key modules: pass or repeat, never a written test.
  • Disclosures go on a separate form only the GBV focal person can open.

The system does on its own

  • Two absences in a row put her on the facilitator's follow-up list.
  • A disclosure creates a referral task for the GBV focal person within 24 hours.
  • A backlash report sends a supervisor to the group before the next session.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Session scripts and picture cards adapted to the local dialect
  • Photographed paper registers read into attendance
  • Monthly patterns in attendance drops by module, season and group

Stage 03 · Savings & support

Is the money hers, and did the referral help?

A woman may be saving, applying for a business loan and seeking legal aid in the same month. What counts is whether the loan's in her name, she controls it, and the referral led somewhere.

Your team does

  • Treasurers keep the paper passbook. Facilitators enter it into a mobile ledger each month.
  • Coordinators track loan and scheme applications until the benefit actually arrives.
  • The GBV focal person logs referrals in a restricted module. Field staff only see that one is open.

The system does on its own

  • Two missed repayments alert the facilitator to talk with the group.
  • No referral follow-up in seven days escalates to the focal person's supervisor.
  • Scheme applications pending over 45 days prompt a check with the block office.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Photographed group ledgers turned into member-level savings
  • Scheme forms pre-filled for the coordinator to check with her
  • Which scheme applications succeed, by block, every quarter

Stage 04 · Prove what shifted

Do women now decide what they couldn't before?

Attendance and loan numbers won't answer the funder's impact question. You need an endline on the same measures as the baseline, and a way to keep tracking women after the programme leaves.

Your team does

  • Enumerators repeat the baseline questions word for word, with the same privacy check.
  • Coordinators grade group readiness on a tablet during a group visit.
  • Federation leaders report roles and collective actions to the M&E officer every quarter.

The system does on its own

  • Each endline links to her own baseline for a paired comparison.
  • Groups below the grading threshold get extended support before graduation.
  • Six- and twelve-month calls to federation leaders schedule themselves.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Baseline to endline comparison by social group, drafted for the report
  • Open answers on collective action coded into themes
  • Federation minutes drafted from voice notes in the local language
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Six connected records: member, group, session, savings ledger, referral, agency survey, with GBV referrals in a restricted layer
  • NRLM, widow pension, Ujjwala and job card applications tracked until the benefit reaches her
  • Agency indicators on income, mobility and voice, fixed at baseline so the endline can be compared
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms and voice prompts in Kannada, Hindi or your language
  • A different screen for the facilitator, the treasurer, the GBV focal person and the manager
  • Follow-ups and escalations that fire on their own, with no automated messages to survivors
  • Each woman gets her balance back, and each group gets its own results
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Women's safety depends on this data. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Choosing who gets enrolled by algorithmThe community's list catches women no record shows.
  • Chatbots answering questions on violenceWrong advice can put her at risk. Trained facilitators handle it.
  • AI credit scores for group loansThey mark women down for the very constraints you're working on.
  • Automated messages to survivorsA message on a shared phone can alert an abuser.
  • AI reading GBV case detailsSurvivor records stay in the restricted layer, with trained staff only.
  • Writing case stories from survey dataHer story is in her own words, shared with her consent.
What changes, and for whom

Everyone enters less and gets something back.

Group leader

Facilitator

Sees who needs a quiet check-in.

Her groups, the next module and the women who've missed two sessions, on one screen.

GBV focal person

Holds the only key to referrals.

Open referrals, days since last contact and safety plans, visible to nobody else.

Programme manager

Sees where support is stuck.

Loans in women's own names, scheme applications by block and groups with backlash reports.

Leadership & funders

Get change they can defend.

Paired baseline to endline on income, mobility and voice, split by social group and tier.

If the baseline was taken with her mother-in-law in the room, the endline shows a fall that isn't real. We record whether every interview was private, and report change on the pairs that were.
