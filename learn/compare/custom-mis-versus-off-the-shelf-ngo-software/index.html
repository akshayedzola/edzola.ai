06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

When should a nonprofit choose a custom MIS instead of off-the-shelf software?

Off-the-shelf software is usually stronger when the workflow is common and the organisation can adopt its model. A custom or low-code MIS is stronger when distinctive journeys, relationships, permissions or reporting logic are central to delivery. The decision should include implementation and long-term ownership.

How do custom and off-the-shelf systems compare on cost and implementation effort?

The main cost and timeline drivers are workflow and permission complexity, data migration quality, integrations, offline or language needs, testing availability, training and long-term ownership. Estimate after modelling one real journey and its exceptions.

How do custom and off-the-shelf systems compare on field work, reporting and permissions?

Off-the-shelf software is usually stronger when the workflow is common and the organisation can adopt its model. A custom or low-code MIS is stronger when distinctive journeys, relationships, permissions or reporting logic are central to delivery. The decision should include implementation and long-term ownership.

Which ownership and exit trade-offs are easy to miss?

The main risks are buying on a polished demo, calling configuration free, ignoring adoption and migration and customising around a broken process. Test these with representative cases and keep the consequential judgement with a named person.

When should a nonprofit replace its current software approach?

Consider switching when critical workflows depend on repeated workarounds, permissions cannot be enforced, reporting requires manual reconstruction or the current platform cannot be maintained responsibly. Test the replacement against a complete real journey before migrating.
