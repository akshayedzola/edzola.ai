One Person ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Find who's missed

Who hasn't been counted yet?

Official figures put disability at about 2% in India. The Washington Group questions usually find several times that, mostly people with psychosocial, intellectual and less visible disabilities, and children kept at home.

Your team does

  • CBR workers ask the Washington Group questions at home, offline, with audio prompts in the local language.
  • Early interventionists screen babies and young children with ASHA, Anganwadi and RBSK teams. Special educators screen in school with teachers and parents.
  • The person answers for themselves first. A family member answers only if they can't.

The system does on its own

  • 'A lot of difficulty' or 'cannot do at all' creates an assessment referral.
  • Children identified who are out of school go straight to the special educator.
  • Villages far below expected identification get a revisit.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A screening form with voice, sign-language video and large text
  • A monthly view of who's missing, by domain, gender and age
  • Easy-read and local-language awareness material

Stage 02 · Assess & plan

What does this person want to do next?

Without a certificate and UDID card, pensions, scholarships, ADIP devices and reservations stay out of reach. The plan that follows has to be built with the person, around goals they chose.

Your team does

  • Therapists record functional assessments on a tablet at camps or the centre.
  • Coordinators track every UDID application and follow up with the medical authority.
  • Plans are made in a meeting with the person, read back in their format, and consent recorded.

The system does on its own

  • A UDID pending over 60 days alerts the coordinator.
  • An issued UDID creates tasks for pension, scholarship and travel concession.
  • Plan reviews land on the rehab team's list two weeks ahead.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A first draft of the plan from their own goals, in plain language and easy-read
  • Schemes each person qualifies for, matched from certificate and profile
  • Where UDID gets stuck, by district and disability type

Stage 03 · Therapy & devices

Is it working for them, at home and on their route?

Therapy works when it's regular and tied to daily life. Devices get abandoned within months when nobody checks fit, training or terrain.

Your team does

  • Therapists log each session with a one-tap rating on the goal worked on.
  • The workshop logs measurement, fabrication and fitting. The workshop logs measurement, fabrication and fitting. The workshop logs measurement, fabrication and fitting. The device team checks fit and use at home at three months.
  • The person or caregiver can report a problem any time by WhatsApp or IVRS.

The system does on its own

  • Fitting a device books its three-month use check.
  • Two missed sessions trigger a call or home visit about barriers.
  • A 'not in use' answer creates a refit or replacement task.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Illustrated, easy-read home exercise programmes, approved by the therapist
  • Caregiver voice notes turned into progress and device updates
  • Captions, screen readers and text-to-speech during training

Stage 04 · Stay included

Are they still there a year later, on their terms?

Placement numbers are easy. Retention is where barriers show: an inaccessible toilet, a teacher who won't adapt, an accommodation agreed at placement and forgotten by month three.

Your team does

  • Placement officers check in at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months, in the person's preferred mode.
  • Access auditors, often persons with disabilities, audit each site with photos of the barriers.
  • Employers and teachers are contacted only with the person's consent.

The system does on its own

  • All four check-ins schedule themselves at placement.
  • An accommodation still missing after 30 days escalates to the inclusion coordinator.
  • Two exits from one employer in a year put it on review before new placements.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Audit findings turned into a costed fix list for each site
  • Which accommodations and employers link to 12-month retention
  • Job options matched to skills and accommodations, for the person to choose from

What disability & inclusion teams ask us for first

Across the life cycle

From the first screening to the first job, one record.

A baby screened at a hospital, a child in inclusive school, a young adult in skill training: often the same person, served by three teams years apart. Each move between programmes is a task with a named owner, so nobody drops off at age six or at age sixteen. Every team sees the history they need and nothing they don't.

  • Early intervention to school at six, school to skilling at sixteen
  • Each handover has an owner and a date
  • Plans follow the person: IFSP, IEP, IRP
  • Consent recorded for what each team can see
Partner NGOs

Twenty-five partners, one set of numbers.

Partner organisations log their own screenings, sessions and devices through their own login, against the same indicators you use. You see plan versus actual by partner, district and programme, with data gaps flagged before the quarter closes. Each partner sees only its own people.

  • Shared indicators across partners
  • Plan versus actual by partner and programme
  • Missing or late data flagged monthly
  • Each partner's records kept separate
Assistive devices

Every device, from the cast to the doorway.

Measurement, fabrication, fitting and adjustments sit on the person's record next to their goals. Three months later the use check tells you whether it's working at home and on their route. Sponsors see devices made, fitted and still in use, with cost per device.

  • Order, measurement, fabrication and fitting dates
  • Use check at three months, refit if needed
  • Cost per device by sponsor
  • Reasons for non-use grouped by model
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Connected records for person, screening, assessment, plan (IFSP, IEP or IRP), session, device, school or job placement, and partner organisation
  • Washington Group and Child Functioning Module screening, RPwD Act disability types, and UDID stored only when the person chooses to share it
  • Indicators on device use, goal progress and 12-month retention, split by disability type and gender
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Accessible forms with voice, sign-language video and large text, offline, in Marathi, Hindi or your language
  • A different screen for the CBR worker, the therapist, the workshop, the placement officer, partner NGOs and the manager
  • Referrals, UDID follow-ups and check-ins that fire on their own
  • The person sees their own goals and progress, in the format they chose
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Nothing about us without us. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Spotting disability from photos or videoDisability is functioning in context. Appearance misses what can't be seen.
  • Estimating disability percentageCertification is a legal, clinical process under the RPwD Act.
  • Setting someone's rehab goalsGoals belong to the person. A model defaults to what's typical.
  • AI sign avatars in place of interpretersThey aren't accurate for Indian sign languages, and the Deaf community hasn't endorsed them.
  • Screening candidates for employersHiring algorithms penalise disability-linked gaps. That's discrimination.
  • Predicting dropout to decide who's placedIt shuts out the people facing the most barriers.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. UDID details, disability type and therapy notes are treated as sensitive personal data, with consent recorded per purpose.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
