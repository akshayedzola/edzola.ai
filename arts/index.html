One Artist ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Open call & jury

Is the call reaching artists beyond the usual circle?

Open calls tend to reward artists who already write proposals in English. If you don't track who applies, from where and in what language, you can't tell whether the call is open.

Your team does

  • Artists apply on a phone-friendly form in text, audio or video, in regional languages.
  • The programme officer screens applications and logs the reason for every rejection.
  • Jurors score on a portal that shows only their assigned, non-conflicted applications.

The system does on its own

  • Every applicant gets an acknowledgement with an application number.
  • Applications are hidden from jurors who've declared a conflict.
  • Outcome and feedback go to every applicant, selected or not.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Audio and regional-language proposals transcribed and translated for jurors
  • The open call drafted in several languages from the approved text
  • Applicant pool patterns summarised across cycles

Stage 02 · Residency & mentoring

Which projects need support, and which tranche is due?

Creative work doesn't move in straight lines. You still need to know tranches went out, mentors showed up and projects are alive, without turning artists into form-fillers.

Your team does

  • The grant record is created from the selected application, with no retyping.
  • Mentors log sessions on a short phone form or a WhatsApp message.
  • Faculty or artist educators take attendance on a phone at the start of each rehearsal or session.

The system does on its own

  • The programme officer is reminded a week before each check-in.
  • Finance is notified when a milestone is logged, so the tranche can go.
  • Learners who miss three rehearsals in a row are flagged.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Grant agreements drafted from the selection record
  • Mentors' voice notes turned into session logs
  • Grantees with no check-in in 60 days surfaced

Stage 03 · Shows & audiences

Who came, who came back, and who haven't we reached?

Festival numbers are easy to inflate and hard to read. Ticketing, free event headcounts and school sessions have to sit together, or you'll only see your paying regulars.

Your team does

  • Box office data syncs from the ticketing platform after each show.
  • Front of house enters the clicker headcount and survey count at close.
  • Artist educators and teachers log each school session on a phone, with attendance and a photo of the children's work.

The system does on its own

  • Opted-in ticket holders get a post-show survey link.
  • An event record still open 10 days after the show alerts the production manager.
  • Schools with no session logged in a month are flagged to the programme manager.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Open-text audience responses grouped by theme
  • Programme notes and press releases drafted in several languages
  • Pricing and scheduling suggestions from past seasons

Stage 04 · Archive & alumni

What did our support do, five years on?

The archive is often a hard drive in a drawer. Alumni careers are the long-term evidence, and they only show up if you ask every year and link the answers to the original grant.

Your team does

  • Artists upload final reports and documentation to a folder linked to their grant.
  • The archivist catalogues assets weekly with credits, dates and rights.
  • The alumni survey goes out each year by email and WhatsApp, linked to the Artist ID.

The system does on its own

  • Grantees are reminded 30 days before and after a final report is due.
  • The alumni survey goes out on each grant's anniversary.
  • Assets with no rights recorded can't be published.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Metadata tags suggested for photos and recordings
  • Public career updates pulled to prompt alumni follow-up
  • Cohort impact reports drafted from alumni and archive data

What arts & culture teams ask us for first

Arts in schools

Show what the arts did for the children, not just how many sessions ran

For arts-in-education programmes, each child's attendance, the work they made and a short baseline and endline on confidence, agency or expression link to the school and the artist educator. The dashboard shows growth by school and facilitator, next to how many children were actually assessed.

  • Session logs with a photo of the work, offline
  • Rubric-based baseline and endline by the facilitator
  • Growth always shown with the number assessed
  • Parental consent recorded before any child's image is stored
Government partnerships

From one teacher training to every classroom that used it

When a state adopts your curriculum, the question becomes whether it's taught. Training batches, classroom observations and resource use over WhatsApp or IVRS sit against each school's UDISE code. The district team sees which blocks are teaching the arts period and which have gone quiet.

  • Training cohorts and cascade trainers by district
  • Classroom observation form with your rubric
  • IVRS and WhatsApp engagement by school
  • District and block views for state reviews
Fellowships

Artist to educator, tracked without turning artists into form-fillers

The same Artist ID carries a fellow from application through training, placement, weekly sessions and learning manager observations, into alumni years. Programme managers see who needs support this month, and fellows see their own sessions, showcases and growth.

  • Recruitment and selection across cycles
  • Training attendance and placement by school or community
  • Session logs and observations in one place
  • Alumni pathways linked back to the cohort
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Connected records for artist, open call, application, grant, fellowship or residency, event, school session and archive asset
  • Every applicant keeps one Artist ID across years, selected or not, with credits and rights on every asset
  • Indicators funders ask for, from first-time applicants and children's growth in confidence to alumni still practising at five years
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Applications in text, audio or video, in regional languages, on a phone
  • A different screen for the juror, the mentor, finance and the director
  • Check-in reminders, tranche alerts and alumni surveys that go out on their own
  • Artists get back their audience numbers and a portfolio page from the archive
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

An artist's work and voice are theirs. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Face recognition on children's photos or videosChildren perform for their school, not for a dataset. Faces are never identified by a model.
  • Scoring or ranking applicationsMerit is the jury's call. A model trained on past picks repeats past gatekeeping.
  • AI-written feedback to applicantsArtists can tell. Feedback comes from what the jurors said.
  • Judging creative progressThat's a conversation between the artist and their mentor.
  • Unreleased work in public AI toolsIt's the artist's property. Uploading it can compromise rights before the premiere.
  • Describing ritual works without the communityMeaning belongs to the practitioners. A model will get it wrong and flatten it.
  • Training AI on your archiveArtists didn't agree to become training data, and rights clearances rarely cover it.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. Parental consent is recorded before any child's image or video is stored.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
