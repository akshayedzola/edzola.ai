One Mother ID + Child ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Register early

Who's pregnant that we haven't registered yet?

A pregnancy registered at 28 weeks has already missed the visits that catch anaemia, hypertension and twins. The risk profile made at registration decides how closely she's followed.

Your team does

  • ASHAs register pregnancies on an offline phone app during home visits.
  • Hospitals enrol walk-ins at the desk, into the same record.
  • A short call confirms whose phone it is and when she can talk privately.

The system does on its own

  • A high-risk flag books an early ANM visit and tells the medical officer.
  • Registration starts voice messages at the right week, in her language and time slot.
  • EDD is worked out from LMP, and delivery planning reaches the ASHA at 32 weeks.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Voice registration in the local language for ASHAs on small screens
  • Likely duplicates flagged when she registers at two homes
  • A monthly note on villages where registration comes late

Stage 02 · ANC that counts

Which mothers need treatment or a birth plan this week?

A visit where nobody measured BP doesn't protect against eclampsia. Anaemia found at 16 weeks can be treated. At 36 weeks it's a bleeding risk.

Your team does

  • ANMs record ANC at the sub-centre, VHSND or urban health post on a tablet, offline, in the same record.
  • ASHAs log danger-sign checks and reminders on their phones during home visits.
  • The voice platform writes back whether each message was heard, and for how long.

The system does on its own

  • Hb under 7 or BP at 140/90 or above alerts the MO and opens a referral.
  • A missed ANC sends a voice reminder, then an ASHA visit 3 days later.
  • At 34 weeks, mothers with no transport arranged go on the ASHA's list.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Mothers likely to stop listening, reached first by the call centre
  • Approved message scripts adapted into new languages
  • A weekly list of visits missing BP, Hb or weight, by facility

Stage 03 · Birth & referral

Did she reach the right facility, and was she seen?

Delays in deciding, reaching and getting care still kill. A referral is only done when she's arrived and been seen, and the birth record opens the child's.

Your team does

  • Labour room nurses record the birth on a tablet, pulling up her ANC record by Mother ID.
  • The referring facility logs the referral with its reason. The receiving one confirms arrival, and whether the referral was the right one, on its own screen.
  • ASHAs log home births within 24 hours, with birth weight if a scale was there.

The system does on its own

  • No arrival 2 hours after a referral? The coordinator gets an alert to call.
  • Each birth creates the Child ID and books newborn visits on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 42.
  • A maternal death or stillbirth notifies the district review team.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • The nearest facility that can manage this complication, suggested
  • A quarterly look at where referral delays happen
  • Draft death review summaries for the review committee

Stage 04 · First 42 days & beyond

Which newborns need a visit today, and who's due a vaccine?

Most newborn deaths happen in the first week, often at home after an early discharge. After that, the vaccine card and growth chart become the child's record.

Your team does

  • ASHAs log newborn home visits offline, with the schedule already loaded from the birth.
  • ANMs record vaccines and weights at VHSND, finding the child by ID or mother's name.
  • Voice messages switch from pregnancy to newborn content once the birth is recorded.

The system does on its own

  • A missed newborn visit day alerts the ASHA and ANM. The first week can't wait.
  • Vaccine reminders go to the mother by voice or SMS and onto the ANM's session list.
  • Two weight drops in a row, or a SAM or MAM reading, create a referral to the anganwadi or NRC.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Which pregnancy gaps link to newborn outcomes, across one cohort
  • Vaccine dates read from a photo of the MCP card
  • Immunisation dropouts ranked by distance and days overdue

What maternal & child health teams ask us for first

Referral networks

A referral is done when she's seen, and the referring nurse knows.

The referring facility logs why she's being sent and calls ahead. The receiving facility confirms arrival and whether the referral was the right one for that level of care. The outcome goes back to the nurse who sent her. Each month, every facility sees its own referral appropriateness and documentation completeness.

  • Pre-referral calls and WhatsApp messages logged against the referral
  • Appropriateness and completeness scored per facility, per month
  • Screens for the nodal person and data person at each facility
  • Unconfirmed arrivals escalated after two hours
Working with government

Your system can stay with the corporation when you step back.

When your job is strengthening health posts and maternity homes, government staff are the main users. We give them simple screens for their own facility, track readiness checks and staff training, and show managers which facilities deliver all core ANC services. When it's time to hand over, the forms, data and admin rights move to the government partner.

  • Facility readiness checklists, including the nine core ANC services
  • Training records per staff member and facility
  • Ward, facility and corporation dashboards from the same data
  • A documented handover of data, forms and admin rights
The first 1,000 days

Link her anaemia in pregnancy to her child's growth at two.

The Child ID opened at birth carries on into anganwadi growth monitoring. Monthly weights, SAM and MAM readings, NRC referrals and recovery sit on the same record as the mother's ANC history. Your team can finally see whether mothers treated for anaemia have children who grow better.

  • Monthly growth monitoring with anganwadi workers, offline
  • SAM and MAM flagged, with NRC referral and follow-up
  • Mother's Hb history visible beside the child's growth chart
  • Stunting and wasting reported with every child enrolled in the base
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Eight connected records: mother, pregnancy, ANC contact, referral, facility, birth outcome, child, growth and postnatal visit
  • Holds her RCH portal ID and follows the MCP card, HBNC, HBYC and immunisation schedules
  • A new pregnancy never overwrites the last, and indicators like first-trimester registration are defined upfront
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Hindi, Marathi or your language, synced at the sub-centre or health post
  • A different screen for the ASHA or community volunteer, the ANM, the MO, the facility nodal officer and the programme manager
  • Visit, referral and vaccine reminders that fire on their own
  • Voice messages timed to her own week, in her language
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Mothers and newborns can't wait for a model to be right. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • High-risk status from a modelRisk criteria come from clinical guidelines, applied by a person.
  • Chatbots judging danger signsA bot can answer everyday questions. Bleeding or reduced movement goes to a nurse or a facility, today.
  • AI in labour decisionsThey take minutes and need a skilled birth attendant.
  • Diagnosis from phone photosUnvalidated anaemia, sepsis or jaundice checks give false comfort.
  • Postpartum depression checks by chatA mother at risk needs a trained person and a clear referral.
  • Mothers' details in public AI toolsNames, phones and health data stay in your private system.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. If she discloses violence, a confidential referral can go to your counselling team without showing on her ANC record.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
