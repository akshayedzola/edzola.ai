Connect pregnancy, risk, referral and follow-up across home and facility.

06 · Common questions

Practical questions teams ask.

Use these answers to clarify scope, compare approaches and identify the decisions your team needs to make before implementation.

How should a nonprofit implement pregnancy and antenatal care tracking systems?

Use a phased sequence: agree timeline and service definitions, link home and facility identity, schedule contacts and referrals, test urgent escalation, reconcile facility confirmation and review maternal and newborn follow-up. Each stage needs a named owner and a review gate before the next stage begins.

What records and data fields are needed for pregnancy and antenatal care tracking systems?

Start with pregnancy care journey. The minimum useful record set covers mother, pregnancy and estimated timeline, frontline visit and ANC contact, risk observation and protocol source, referral, facility and urgency, service confirmation and next due action and birth, postnatal and closure status. Remove any field that does not support a service, safeguard, decision or reporting obligation.

Which staff roles and review steps should pregnancy and antenatal care tracking systems include?

Give every stage a named operational owner and require a separate human review for clinical risk assessment, emergency response, changing the care plan and closing a pregnancy record after loss or transfer. Use ANC contacts completed on time, urgent referrals acknowledged, facility confirmation and postnatal follow-up completed to decide whether the workflow is ready to expand.

What are the most common mistakes when setting up pregnancy and antenatal care tracking systems?

The main risks are duplicate pregnancies across workers, risk alerts without response capacity, estimating clinical status from incomplete data and unsafe disclosure on shared phones. Test these with representative cases and keep the consequential judgement with a named person.

How should pregnancy and antenatal care tracking systems change for a small NGO versus a multi-state programme?

Keep the definition of pregnancy care journey stable at every scale. A small programme can begin with one workflow and a few roles; a multi-state programme also needs delegated ownership, stronger permissions, shared definitions, integration rules and location-level quality review.
