What a nonprofit receives

The Digital Transformation Grant

Not a software licence. A structured piece of work worth about $2,500, around 120 days long, followed by a year of support. The nonprofit puts in a small share of its own.

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Discover

    We map how money, people and data actually move today, and agree what "better" looks like.

  2. Weeks 3–4

    Design

    One high-value system, scoped to what the team can adopt. Prototype first, then sign-off.

  3. Weeks 5–10

    Build

    Configured on proven templates, with their real data migrated in.

  4. Weeks 11–16

    Adopt

    Field staff trained in their language. We don't leave until the team is actually using it.

  5. Day 120 + 12 months

    Measure

    Baseline against after. Support for a year, and licence costs planned before go-live so nothing breaks in year two.

The nonprofit doesn't pick software.

We diagnose the organisation and build the single change that will matter most and that we can deliver well. Sometimes that's a CRM. Sometimes it's a field app that replaces paper forms.

The conflict, said plainly

EdZola runs this fund and is paid to deliver the work.

You should know that up front. Here's how we keep it honest.

  • An independent committee chooses the nonprofits. EdZola staff can't outvote it.
  • Organisations already paying EdZola aren't eligible. This is for those who can't afford us.
  • Our rates are published and benchmarked against other implementation partners.
  • Each nonprofit signs off its own before-and-after figures.
  • Once a year we publish the fund's books: money in, sponsor fees, delivery cost, matched hours.
  • No lock-in. Nonprofits own their data and system, and aren't required to buy anything from us afterwards.

I started EdZola in 2020, in the middle of lockdown, after years as an engineer at Zoho and a classroom teacher with Teach For India.

Since then we've built systems for more than a hundred nonprofits. The ones that could pay us got better at their work. The ones that couldn't kept running remarkable programmes out of spreadsheets and WhatsApp, and we kept saying no to them.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

For US donors, yes. Gifts will go through a US 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor that owns the fund, which is the arrangement we're finalising now. For UK donors we're setting up a Gift Aid route. Until the fund opens, a pledge is non-binding and no money changes hands.

Do I get to choose the nonprofit?

No, and that's deliberate. An independent committee selects nonprofits against published criteria, and the fund decides how money is spent. You're then matched with one of the transformations your cohort paid for. It keeps the selection fair and keeps your gift deductible.

Why not just give the money to the nonprofits directly?

Two reasons. Many small Indian nonprofits aren't registered to receive money from abroad. And cash alone rarely produces a working system; the hard part is the design, migration and training. The fund pays for that delivered work, and the nonprofit pays only a small share of its own.

Does EdZola make money from this?

EdZola is paid for delivery at its published nonprofit rates and adds matched hours on every project at no charge. The yearly report shows exactly how much went to delivery, fees and matched time.

What happens after 120 days?

Each nonprofit gets 12 months of support. Before go-live we agree how licence and running costs will be covered in year two, so the system doesn't fall over when free credits end.

Which countries?

The first cohort is in India. Kenya follows in the second cohort, with other East African countries after we've tested the model there.

Can I pay in pounds?

Yes. UK Patrons pledge the pound equivalent of $1,000. We'll confirm the route and exact amount when the fund opens.
