Procurement, purchasing & inventory systems

Follow every purchase from need to receipt, payment and stock.

We design connected procurement systems for mission-led and commercial organisations: purchase requests, budget checks, vendors, quotations, approvals, purchase orders, goods or service acceptance, invoices, payments, inventory and audit trails.

What breaks today

A purchase loses control when each team sees only one part.

Email, spreadsheets and accounting tools can each hold a fragment without showing the full commitment, the current exception or who needs to act next.

01 · APPROVAL FOG

Requests move, but accountability does not.

Approvals sit across email and chat, with no dependable queue, owner, rejection reason or history.

02 · LATE COMMITMENTS

The budget is checked after the decision.

Backdated requests and late purchase orders make it difficult to see committed spend before cash leaves.

03 · BROKEN TRACEABILITY

Receipt, invoice and stock do not share one reference.

Teams reconcile goods, services, bills, payments, assets and stock by hand because the chain breaks after the PO.

Request-to-reconciliation

One traceable chain, with the right control at each handoff.

The exact policy differs by organisation. The underlying records and decisions repeat.

  1. Need and purchase request
  2. Budget, policy and sourcing route
  3. Vendor, quotations and selection
  4. Approval and purchase order
  5. Receipt or service acceptance
  6. Invoice, payment and reconciliation

For physical goods, the chain continues into stock, warehouse or asset custody. For services, acceptance can be a milestone, timesheet or completion confirmation.

Configurable controls

Make policy executable without hard-coding today’s org chart.

Thresholds, roles, states and documents should be configurable so the workflow can change without rebuilding the application.

Request and budget context

Purpose, entity, programme, project, grant or cost centre, budget head, quantity, dates, delivery location and supporting evidence.

Vendor and quotation governance

Controlled vendor onboarding and changes, compliance documents, categories, prices, lead times, quotation comparison and selection rationale.

Approval matrix and segregation

Role-, department- and value-based routes, delegated authority, no self-approval, rejection reasons, resubmission and a visible decision history.

PO and commitment control

Numbering, terms, advances, credit periods, amendments, versions, partial fulfilment, committed value, remaining balance and formal closure.

Goods and services

Use one control model without pretending every purchase is the same.

The request, sourcing, approval and commitment records can be shared. Acceptance evidence must follow what was actually bought.

Two paths after the PO.

A clean design branches at fulfilment, then rejoins for invoice validation and payment.

01

Physical goods

Quantity and quality checks, goods receipt note, batch or serial details where needed, returns, warehouse/location, stock or asset update.

02

Services and milestones

Deliverable, milestone, period or timesheet confirmation, accountable service owner, exceptions and evidence of acceptance.

03

Invoice control

Match the invoice to the approved PO and receipt or acceptance, including partial invoices, advances, taxes and balance checks.

04

Accounting handoff

Send validated records to the authorised finance process or tools such as Zoho Books, Zoho Expense or Tally, with reconciliation status.

Inventory, warehouse & assets

Receipt is not the end of the process.

When the organisation buys physical goods, purchasing must connect to custody, movement, valuation and reconciliation.

Item and location masters

Categories, SKUs, variants, units of measure, preferred vendors, warehouses, locations, reorder rules and responsible users.

Goods-in and stock book

PO-linked receipts, grading or quality, accepted and rejected quantity, opening stock, goods in, goods out and closing balance.

Transfers and adjustments

Warehouse transfers, packaging or unit conversion, wastage, returns, stock-taking differences and authorised corrections.

Operational reporting

Pending approvals, cycle time, committed versus actual spend, open POs, receipt variance, stock position, ageing, reorder and vendor performance.

Part of Finance & Operations

Start with procurement; connect the organisation in sequence.

A PR–PO workflow can remove pressure quickly. It also creates a reliable bridge between budgets, projects, grants, people, assets, inventory and accounting—making it a strong stage in organisation-wide digital transformation.
