Fundraising, donor & grant systems

Follow every relationship and rupee from first conversation to final report.

Fundraising CRM, donor management, donation processing and grant management are related, but they are not the same job. We connect them without blurring ownership, so relationship history, commitments, money and programme evidence stay aligned.

What breaks today

The relationship, the award and the actual spend live in different worlds.

A CRM can show the pipeline and still leave grant obligations, donation allocation and utilisation in spreadsheets. A useful system makes each boundary explicit.

01 · LOST CONTEXT

Relationships depend on one person’s inbox.

Meeting history, proposals, next steps and institutional memory disappear when ownership changes.

02 · BROKEN HANDOFF

Closed-won is where the spreadsheet begins.

The award moves from fundraising to programmes and finance without one shared grant record or reporting calendar.

03 · REPORTING SCRAMBLE

Budgets, actuals and evidence are reconciled too late.

Teams rebuild utilisation and narrative reports from multiple sources close to the deadline.

One funding lifecycle

Six stages, with clear system boundaries.

The system should preserve the full relationship without pretending CRM, payment processing, accounting and programme evidence are one application.

  1. Prospect and relationship
  2. Opportunity and proposal
  3. Award or donation
  4. Grant, budget and tranches
  5. Delivery, actuals and evidence
  6. Reporting, renewal or closure
Fundraising CRM

A pipeline your team can trust because it captures the relationship behind it.

Move from scattered contacts and follow-ups to a shared view of organisations, people, opportunities, proposals, activities and forecasts.

Prospects and relationships

Organisations, contacts, relationship owners, interests, giving history, consent and a complete activity timeline.

Opportunities and proposals

Qualification, stages, asks, proposal versions, probabilities, next steps, deadlines and pipeline forecasts.

Stewardship and renewal

Communication plans, commitments, acknowledgements, updates, engagement and timely renewal signals.

Leadership visibility

Pipeline health, concentration risk, movement, ageing, conversion and forecast views that trace back to records.

Donor & donation management

Process gifts cleanly without losing the person behind them.

Donation operations sit between relationship management, payment channels, finance and communication. We design the handoffs so one gift does not create four conflicting records.

01

Capture and reconcile

Bring donation, payment reference, channel, campaign and donor identity together with exception handling.

02

Allocate and acknowledge

Connect designations and funds to acknowledgements or receipts, subject to the organisation’s approved compliance process.

03

Segment and steward

Use giving history, interests, engagement and consent to plan relevant communication and retention.

Grant & fund management

Turn an award into a managed set of obligations.

A grant record should connect agreement terms to budgets, tranches, deliverables, reporting dates, restrictions, programme evidence and actual spend.

Agreement and obligations

Fund, donor, period, geography, programme, restrictions, deliverables, documents and accountable owners.

Budgets and tranches

Approved budget structure, revisions, conditions, expected receipts, tranche status and dependencies.

Utilisation and variance

Accounting handoffs, allocations, budget-versus-actual views, exceptions and evidence for review.

Reporting calendar

Narrative and financial reports, programme indicators, supporting documents, approvals, submissions and renewal.

Part of organisation-wide transformation

Funding systems work when programmes and finance are designed into the handoff.

We can begin with the fundraising pipeline, then connect donations, grant obligations, programme evidence and accounting actuals in a planned sequence. Each release removes a real source of pressure rather than waiting for one giant launch.
