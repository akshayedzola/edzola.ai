DPDP, in plain words

What the law actually asks of you.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's data privacy law. If your organisation keeps any personal information digitally (beneficiary records, donor lists, volunteer details, student data), it applies to you. Here's what it expects, translated for the social sector.

01

Ask before you collect

You need informed consent, or another valid legal basis, before collecting personal data. For anyone under 18, that means verifiable consent from a parent or guardian. A student filling in a form is not consent from their parent.

02

Tell people why

Every collection point (a field app, a paper form, a website) must carry a clear notice: what you're collecting, why, and who to contact. In language your beneficiaries actually understand.

03

Use it only for that purpose

Data collected for attendance can't quietly become a fundraising list. Collect the minimum you need, use it for the stated purpose, and nothing more.

04

Honour people's rights

Beneficiaries, donors and volunteers can ask to see their data, correct it or have it erased, and raise a grievance if you don't respond. You need a working way to receive and answer these requests, not just good intentions.

05

Guard it, and let it go

Reasonable security safeguards are mandatory, data breaches must be reported, and data must be deleted once its purpose is served. "We keep everything forever, just in case" is now a liability, not diligence.

06

Own what you share

Sharing with government departments, funders, vendors or tech providers? You remain responsible. That means written agreements and oversight for everyone who touches your data on your behalf.

₹250 cr

Who enforces this? The Data Protection Board of India, with penalties of up to ₹250 crore per instance for serious failures. There is no exemption for nonprofits. But compliance isn't really about avoiding penalties. It's about being worthy of the trust your communities already place in you.

Questions we hear most

Straight answers, in plain words.

Does DPDP really apply to small NGOs?

Yes. The Act applies to every organisation in India that handles personal data digitally, whatever its size or legal form. There is no exemption for nonprofits. If you keep beneficiary records, donor lists or volunteer details on a computer, a phone or in the cloud, it applies to you.

What happens in the 15-minute assessment?

A voice AI interview asks about the systems you use, the data you collect and how it moves: field apps, paper forms, spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups and partners. It only asks what's relevant to your answers. Within two weeks you get your data map, a scored risk report and a prioritised 180-day action plan.

Who sees our data during the assessment?

The assessment asks how you handle data, not for the data itself. We never see or hold your beneficiary or donor records, and all personal data stays within your own environment. The report is yours.

What if we can't afford the full compliance work?

Start with Assess, from ₹10,000 based on your team size. Your action plan is prioritised, so you'll know which fixes matter most and which your own team can take on. There's no lock-in, and if you do continue to Comply within 60 days, the assessment fee is credited.

What do funders actually ask for?

More funders and CSR partners now ask grantees how they protect the personal data of the people they serve: consent, retention, data-sharing agreements and who has access. A scored assessment and an action plan with owners and timelines give you a clear, documented answer.
