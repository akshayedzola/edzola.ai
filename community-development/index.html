Five places, one village. Nobody can see the tank has stalled.

Sound familiar?

Your villages run on trust. Your records are in six places.

These come up in almost every first conversation we have with community development teams.

"The hamlet past the river had nothing in the plan. Nobody noticed."

Needs from the PRA sit on chart paper. By sign-off, no one can say whose priority got dropped, or why.

Programme coordinator
"I find out a work has stalled when the mason calls."

Inspection photos are on five phones. A site with no progress stays invisible until the season's nearly over.

Programme engineer
"Contributions were agreed. Half were never collected."

Pledges are in one notebook, payments in the treasurer's cash book. The O&M fund is empty on handover day.

Programme manager

After handover, follow-up depends on who remembers. Nobody can say how many pumps still run.

One Village ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Reach every hamlet

Has every household and hamlet been reached?

If you only meet the sarpanch and the families near the main road, the Dalit hamlet and landless households get left out of everything that follows.

Your team does

  • Mobilisers enumerate every house on a shared phone, offline, in Odia, Hindi or your language.
  • Attendance is logged at the end of each hamlet meeting, with a photo of the sheet.
  • Committee members are linked to their Household IDs after the election meeting.

The system does on its own

  • A hamlet with no meeting in the first month is flagged to the mobiliser.
  • A committee missing women or a hamlet is flagged before it's confirmed.
  • Enumeration under 95% of the Census estimate holds the village back from planning.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Handwritten attendance sheets read into counts by gender and hamlet
  • Enumeration checked against Census, JJM and SBM figures
  • A hamlet-wise household map drawn for the committee

Stage 02 · Plan with the village

Did every hamlet's priority make it into the plan?

PRA charts end up rolled in a cupboard. When the plan drops a hamlet's top need without saying why, people stop coming to meetings.

Your team does

  • Mobilisers log each need after a PRA session, with a photo of the chart or map.
  • The gram sabha's decision on each need is entered the same day, with the resolution photo.
  • Engineers build estimates and link every planned work to the needs it answers.

The system does on its own

  • A need marked 'dropped' can't be saved without a reason.
  • Hamlets with nothing in the plan are flagged before sign-off.
  • Once the plan is approved, each work lands in the works tracker with milestones.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Gram sabha recordings turned into a draft list of decisions
  • Raw PRA needs grouped, with duplicates across hamlets linked
  • A first draft of the village development plan

Stage 03 · Build and account

Which works need attention, and where did the money go?

Grants, contributions, MGNREGA and JJM money all arrive at different times. Without works tied to a fund ledger, the committee can't show the village where the money went.

Your team does

  • Engineers log site inspections offline, with GPS-stamped photos.
  • Treasurers enter money in and out on the app, or the accountant copies the cash book weekly.
  • Mobilisers update each scheme application after every block office visit.

The system does on its own

  • No inspection photo for the milestone? The payment request is blocked.
  • No progress in 21 days alerts the engineer and the coordinator.
  • A scheme application pending 60 days becomes a follow-up task.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Bills and cash book pages read into the fund ledger
  • Inspection photos compared to flag works that look unchanged
  • A plain-language monthly works and funds summary for the committee

Stage 04 · Hand over & follow up

Is it still working, and can the village pay for the next repair?

Assets fail when the O&M fund runs dry, the operator moves away or the committee stops meeting. Follow-up visits show which villages need a hand before it breaks.

Your team does

  • Handover MoUs are scanned into the village record at signing.
  • Treasurers send the monthly O&M balance by WhatsApp or IVRS, and the mobiliser enters it.
  • Mobilisers log follow-up visits with asset photos and a short checklist.

The system does on its own

  • Follow-up visits schedule themselves at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months.
  • An asset down for more than 7 days alerts the engineer and the panchayat contact.
  • A fund balance below one major repair flags the village for a committee meeting.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • IVRS check-ins with treasurers and pump operators
  • Patterns in what separates working assets from broken ones
  • The endline report drafted, by village and hamlet
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Seven connected records: village, household, committee, need, work, fund entry, scheme linkage
  • Villages keyed to LGD codes, with plan works ready to align with the Gram Panchayat's GPDP
  • JJM, SBM, PMAY and MGNREGA applications tracked to sanction, with the indicators your funder asks for
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Odia, Hindi or your language, synced at the block office
  • A different screen for the mobiliser, the engineer, the committee and the manager
  • Payment holds, visit reminders and follow-ups that fire on their own
  • Each hamlet gets back its own list of what it asked for and what was decided
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Villages own the plan. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Ranking village prioritiesThat's the gram sabha's decision. A model's guess would replace their voice.
  • Choosing villages by AI score aloneReadiness depends on willingness and relationships that no dataset holds.
  • Social group data in public AI toolsIt's sensitive and can be misused. It stays in your private system.
  • Safety sign-off from a photoA tank needs a qualified engineer on site. A photo can't show a weak foundation.
  • Deciding who gets a scheme applicationEligibility has exceptions that need someone who knows the family and the scheme.
  • Dropping follow-up for 'likely failures'Those are the villages that need the most support.
