One Patient ID, four moments

At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Find every case

Which houses haven't we reached?

Clinics catch the people well enough to walk in. The rest cough at home for months. Door-to-door screening closes that gap, if the register shows which houses you've actually covered.

Your team does

  • CHWs screen door to door on an offline phone form, synced at the sub-centre.
  • Supervisors review the coverage map every Friday and reassign unvisited clusters.
  • Camp teams enter X-ray results at the van, on the same Patient ID.

The system does on its own

  • A presumptive case creates a sample pickup task, due within 48 hours.
  • Household contacts of a newly diagnosed patient appear on the CHW's list for screening.
  • Villages under 70% coverage at month-end are flagged to the supervisor.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • CHWs speak the screening form in the local language instead of typing
  • A weekly summary of coverage gaps and low-yield villages
  • The monthly screening report drafted in the district's HMIS format

Stage 02 · Start treatment fast

Who's been diagnosed but hasn't started treatment?

A sample leaves the village, the result comes back positive, and nobody tells the patient. Every day between diagnosis and the first dose is another day of infection at home.

Your team does

  • CHWs log sample pickup, and a scan at the lab records transit time.
  • Lab technicians enter results, or they sync from the national lab system where that's possible.
  • Treatment coordinators work a daily call list of new positives.

The system does on its own

  • A positive result alerts the patient's CHW and the coordinator on the app and WhatsApp.
  • No result after 5 days escalates to the lab supervisor.
  • A drug-resistant result creates a referral to the DR-TB centre.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A daily call list of patients not yet started, ranked by delay
  • Referral letters drafted from the record, for the MO to sign
  • A monthly note on where patients drop off between test and treatment

Stage 03 · Keep them on treatment

Who's missing doses this week?

A six-month course feels endless by week eight, when symptoms fade and side effects don't. Missed doses usually trace back to travel cost, lost wages, nausea or stigma.

Your team does

  • Treatment supporters log each dose in under 30 seconds, offline, with a one-tap reason for a missed one.
  • Patients who self-report by toll-free call or IVRS sync to the same record.
  • Counsellors log sessions and support, like nutrition kits, during visits.

The system does on its own

  • Two missed doses in a row send an SMS and create a home visit for the supporter.
  • A serious side effect alerts the MO at once, with the regimen and history.
  • The end of the intensive phase books the follow-up sputum test.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Early signs a patient is drifting, before three doses are missed
  • Counsellors' voice notes turned into recorded barriers
  • Reminder scripts and side-effect FAQs in regional languages

Stage 04 · Record every outcome

Does every patient have an outcome?

A success rate only means something if everyone enrolled gets an outcome. 'Not evaluated' and 'lost to follow-up' are where programmes hide their failures.

Your team does

  • CHWs log tracing visits with location, and a photo only where consent allows.
  • Supervisors log phone tracing and transfers to other districts.
  • The MO records outcomes, and an outcome can't be saved without the end-of-treatment test.

The system does on its own

  • Seven days with no dose opens a tracing case, escalating at day 14.
  • Past the expected end date with no outcome? The MO gets the list every Monday.
  • Patients who complete get check visits booked at 6 and 12 months.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A quarterly plain-language read on which blocks and barriers link to worse outcomes
  • Tracing cases ordered so CHWs start with those still reachable
  • The funder outcome report drafted from cohort data

What health & wellbeing teams ask us for first

Many programmes, one person

The factory screen, the clinic visit and the scheme desk are the same person.

Most health organisations run several programmes for several funders, and each one keeps its own sheet. We build one person record underneath them all. Each programme keeps its own forms and indicators, and each funder or brand sees only the sites and people they pay for.

  • Programme, site and funder tagged on every record
  • Brand and funder views limited to their own factories or sites
  • Household details and scheme status entered once, used everywhere
  • Consent recorded per programme, not once for everything
BP and sugar, followed through

You screened 2,000 workers. How many have their BP under control?

A single camp reading doesn't tell you much. The system books a confirmation reading, tracks who started medicine at your clinic or the government NCD clinic, reminds people before a refill runs out, and records BP or sugar at 3 and 6 months. The control rate keeps everyone who screened positive in the base, including the ones you couldn't reach.

  • Point-of-care readings logged against the person, not the camp
  • Refill and review dates that book themselves
  • Controlled rate with the unreached shown as their own number
  • Referrals to PHC or NCD clinic tracked to the first visit
Community-owned programmes

Collectives see their own members. Nobody else sees who they are.

When a sex worker collective or a women's federation runs the programme, its leaders need their own screen: members, dues, health product sales, scheme applications and follow-ups due. Identities of sex workers, trans persons and people living with HIV stay visible only to the outreach staff named for them. Everyone else sees counts.

  • Membership, fees and product sales per collective
  • Community-led monitoring scorecards logged against each facility
  • Role-based masking of key population identities
  • Every record exportable, in your formats, whenever you want it
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Seven connected records: household, person, screening, test result, care plan, follow-up or dose log, health worker
  • Nikshay IDs, ABHA numbers and NCD portal IDs stored alongside yours, so you can match government data without entering it twice
  • Indicators from screened to cured or controlled, with denominators, defined before the first form goes out
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Hindi, Bhojpuri, Odia or your language, synced at the sub-centre
  • A different screen for the CHW, the supervisor, the MO and the manager
  • Sample pickups, home visits and tracing cases that start on their own
  • The CHW sees the outcome for every case she found
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • Six tested use cases, picked for the hours they save
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

Health records are the most sensitive data you hold. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Patient data in public AI toolsNames, locations and diagnoses stay in a private system, with consent.
  • Reading lab reports with general AIResults need validated tools and a clinician.
  • AI choosing a regimen or doseThat's the MO's call, under national guidelines.
  • Labelling patients 'non-compliant'It blames the patient and hides the real barrier.
  • Assigning outcomes automaticallyCured or completed needs test evidence and a clinician.
  • Predicting who'll fail to ration careThe people struggling most need more support.

Your data, your rules

What 'your data stays yours' actually means.

The questions your finance head and IT lead will ask, answered before the call.

◎

Your account, your licence

Zoho licences are bought in your organisation's name and hosted in the Zoho data centre region you choose, including India. We work inside your account.

⇩

Export any time

Every record exports to Excel or CSV whenever you want, including the day you stop working with us. You're not locked in to us for support either.

◐

Access by role

Each person sees only the records their role needs. Identities of key populations are masked by role. Employers and brands see totals, never a worker's name.

≡

A trail on every change

Edits are logged with who made them and when, so an audit or a funder query doesn't start from memory.

✓

Consent and DPDP

Consent is recorded per purpose, with retention and deletion rules agreed up front. ZolaShield, our DPDP platform, shows where you stand.

✦

AI stays inside your system

AI features run on your records under your account's controls. Beneficiary records never go into public AI tools.
