At every stage, the system answers one question your team is already asking.

Pick a stage. You'll see what your team does, what the system handles on its own, and what shows up on the manager's screen.

Stage 01 · Find the poorest

Which households did the village list miss?

The poorest families are often left out of village meetings, SHGs and government lists. Wealth ranking with the community and a verification visit find them, before any money is spent.

Your team does

  • CRPs record wealth ranking on a shared phone after the village meeting, offline, with a photo of the social map.
  • Field officers run the PPI or Stoplight survey on home visits, in the local language.
  • A programme officer verifies on a separate form, without seeing the first answers.

The system does on its own

  • Where the ranking and the score disagree, a verification visit comes first.
  • A duplicate check on household head, village and location stops a family enrolling twice.
  • Once a household is confirmed, the woman is linked to her group on her record.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Voice notes from ranking meetings sorted into household categories
  • Villages where ranking, score and visit disagree, shown side by side
  • The PPI survey adapted into Hindi, Odia or Swahili with local examples

Stage 02 · Build the habit

Who's ready for an asset, and which groups need more help?

A goat given to a woman who hasn't saved or planned is often sold within months. Group savings and a plan she believes in are what make the asset stick.

Your team does

  • CRPs enter each weekly meeting on a phone, matching the paper register.
  • Field officers mark training attendance at the end of every session.
  • Business plans are filled in with the participant, with a photo of her hand-drawn plan.

The system does on its own

  • No meeting logged for 14 days alerts the CRP and the field officer.
  • Finish the modules with an approved plan, and she joins the asset queue.
  • Savings falling three meetings running puts the group on the visit list.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • A photo of the group register read into savings, loans and repayments
  • A spoken business idea turned into a one-page plan with costs
  • IVRS money lessons in the local language, with short quizzes

Stage 03 · Track the asset

Where did every rupee and every goat go?

Asset transfer is where the biggest money moves, and where things go missing. A clean handover record shows funders where the money went and tells mentors which enterprises need help early.

Your team does

  • Coordinators log each purchase at the market, with vendor and price.
  • Field officers record handover at the home with a photo, location and acknowledgement, offline if needed.
  • Finance matches payments against purchases and handovers every week.

The system does on its own

  • A handover with no photo or acknowledgement goes to finance for review.
  • Every asset transfer books a mentor visit within 14 days.
  • A stipend missing from the payment file after 5 days alerts the programme officer.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Passbook and mobile money screenshots read to confirm stipends
  • Purchase, handover and payment records matched to find gaps before audit
  • A monthly summary of assets transferred and early problems

Stage 04 · Coach to graduation

Which households can stand on their own?

Regular mentor visits are what make this a graduation programme. Visit data shows who's on track, and a fair assessment tells you whether the household can manage without you.

Your team does

  • Mentors log each home visit on a phone, offline, with a photo of the asset.
  • Mentors go through their household list every Monday at the block office.
  • Graduation is checked by an officer who isn't the household's mentor, on a fixed checklist.

The system does on its own

  • A household not visited in 30 days goes on the mentor's priority list.
  • An asset marked sold or dead triggers a programme officer review within a week.
  • Meeting every criterion on two visits running puts a household up for assessment.

Where AI helps at this stage

  • Voice-note visit logs turned into savings, income and asset fields
  • Households whose savings or income fell two visits running, flagged
  • Cohort graduation reports drafted by village and mentor
How we build it · the MAP approach

We fix the data first. AI comes last, and it's better for it.

Most AI projects stall because the records underneath are messy. So we work in a fixed order.

M
Model

Start with the decisions you need to make.

Every field has to earn its place by helping someone decide something. If nobody uses it, we don't collect it.

  • Seven connected records: household, participant, group, enterprise, disbursement, mentor visit, graduation check
  • SHG and NRLM or VSLA group IDs stored alongside, so you can match against government records
  • Graduation criteria and income indicators agreed with your funder before the first form goes out
A
Align

Fit it to how your field team already works.

A system people avoid is worse than a spreadsheet. We design around shared phones, patchy signal and WhatsApp.

  • Offline forms in Odia, Hindi, Bengali or your language
  • A different screen for the CRP, the mentor, finance and the manager
  • Visit reminders and handover checks that fire on their own
  • Groups get a monthly savings statement, on WhatsApp or on paper
P
Power with AI

Then add AI where it saves real hours.

On clean, connected records, AI can read paperwork, draft reports and flag who needs attention. A person always checks.

  • 12 tested use cases across the four stages
  • A human check written into every one
  • Clear lines on where AI doesn't belong

Where you start depends on where you are. If your records are already in good shape, we can move to AI quickly. If they're in notebooks, we start there, and we don't start from zero.

Offline forms, local languages, government ID mapping and AI features are configured to each programme's needs and consent rules, so not every build includes all of them.

Where we won't use AI.

These are the poorest families in the village. These lines don't move, whoever's asking.

  • Deciding eligibility without a visitSatellite or phone data misses the widow living in a relative's house.
  • Rejecting at a score cut-offA household one point above the line may be in crisis.
  • Automated grant or loan decisionsThe mentor knows the household. A person owns that call.
  • Fewer visits for 'likely failures'The households struggling most need more coaching.
  • Automated graduationGraduation ends support. Someone who knows the family decides.
  • Financial records in public AI toolsNames and account numbers stay in your private system.
What changes, and for whom

Everyone enters less and gets something back.

Participant

Sees how far she's come.

An asset card for what she received, and a progress card showing which graduation criteria she's met.

CRP

Gets books members trust.

A monthly statement of savings, loans and balances for each group, and the final eligible list to explain to the village.

Mentor

Walks in knowing what's changed.

What's different since the last visit and the actions agreed, before she reaches the door.

Programme officer

Sees who's close and who's slipping.

Households near graduation, those falling behind, and visit coverage by mentor.

Leadership & funders

Get graduation numbers that hold.

Graduation by cohort, income and savings change, and a check 12 months after support ends.

Households marked graduated to hit a target often slide back within a year. We have graduation checked by someone other than the mentor, go back 12 months later, and report both numbers.

Funders notice. It's the kind of honesty that gets you renewed.
